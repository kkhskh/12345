# 1. ESTABLISHED FINDINGS AND SCIENTIFIC LIMITS

Investigation XIII-C reported four confirmed and four falsified frozen predictions.

Important findings included:

1. Across controlled initialization/data-order comparisons, the final neural organization followed initialization more strongly than training-data order.

2. The fresh readout map \(W_A\), of shape \(64\times3\), emerged as the leading candidate for controlling the organization.

3. The original frozen prediction that fresh layer-2 initialization carried the effect was falsified.

4. One post-review exploratory comparison favored \(W_A\) over both layer-2 initialization and the data stream.

5. Temporarily masking an early-readout pathway or a layer-2 mixture pathway changed the final neural organization, with effects persisting after the mask was removed.

6. The mathematical power-mean parameter \(\rho\) was shown to admit an exactly equivalent interpretation as modified target-question evidence followed by output sharpening.

7. The observed \(\rho\) values were strongly associated with overall model error, weakening the claim that \(\rho\) uniquely identifies a distinct internal combination operator.

8. Successful early-layer adaptation for partial-copying two-colluder decisions produced a low-dimensional activation region that causally transferred much of the decision effect.

9. That activation region was poorly aligned with the previously tested linear decoder directions.

10. Supplying the relevant joint-source information at the final layer allowed the final-layer repair to succeed, provided it received sufficient optimization.

11. The earlier claim that the information must be supplied in a specific per-group format was withdrawn.

12. Improved causal controls exposed source-independent patching artifacts and important differences between attention-row slope and intervention fidelity.

These results are useful, but they do not yet establish a complete causal explanation.

## Remaining critical weaknesses

The independent review identified several limitations that this investigation must confront:

- The cleanest \(W_A\)-specific confirmation was exploratory and came from one parent.
- Initialization and data effects were not exhaustively separated at the component level.
- Masking demonstrated lasting changes in the selected solution, but did not distinguish active competition from downstream commitment.
- One masking run trained poorly.
- The proposed early organization predictor failed near the class boundary.
- The \(\rho\) interpretation is confounded with accuracy and calibration.
- The three-dimensional repair representation was tested for sufficiency but not through a matched necessity test.
- Linear decoder directions were compared with a learned causal subspace using mismatched sites and potentially inappropriate encoding directions.
- The learned subspace was not adequately tested against null and irrelevant donor differences.
- Availability of relational information was established more strongly than its naturally learned implementation.
- The broad eligibility intervention site contained the edited source tokens themselves.
- The supposed parallel pathways may be a decomposition of one distributed mechanism rather than independent algorithms.
- Some attention-head controls measured donor-pair specificity rather than genuine mechanism specificity.
- The full 4,000-step deterministic training replay was not completed.
- No internal predictor of the behavioral learning transition survived.

**These are the starting obligations of XIII-D, not optional future suggestions.**

# 2. FOUR PRIMARY SCIENTIFIC HYPOTHESES

Investigate four competing sets of explanations.

### H1 — Selection by readout initialization

The fresh readout map influences early gradient flow and thereby biases which computational pathway the model learns.

Alternative explanations include:

- Interactions between the readout map and training data.
- Joint dependence on multiple freshly initialized components.
- General optimization sensitivity unrelated to an identifiable readout property.
- A broader initialization-dependent feature not captured by the current explanation.

### H2 — Active competition versus passive commitment

One internal pathway may suppress another during training.

Alternatively, downstream components may simply commit to whichever pathway becomes useful first, without active suppression.

A third possibility is that both processes occur under different training conditions.

### H3 — Causal repair through a transformed joint representation

Successful two-colluder repair may construct a low-dimensional representation used by the frozen final layer.

However, that representation might encode:

- A nonlinear function of the correct copying statistic.
- Modified source-reliability weights.
- An attention-key-space representation.
- Candidate-specific evidence corrections.
- A broader sufficient transformation.
- An intervention artifact optimized for interchange.

### H4 — Apparent mechanisms depend on intervention design

Some claimed mediators may arise from:

- Learned alignment objectives.
- Synthetic activation construction.
- Source-free base-rate effects.
- Mismatched neural sites.
- Linear decoder versus encoding-direction confusion.
- Nearly linear decompositions of distributed computation.

All four hypothesis families must be investigated with discriminating tests.

# 4. WORKSTREAM B — ACTIVE PATHWAY COMPETITION OR LEARNING COMMITMENT?

**Mandatory.**

The previous masking experiments established path dependence.

They did not establish that neural pathways actively compete during unmodified training.

This distinction is fundamental.

## B1. Build explicit competing mechanistic predictions

Consider:

**Competition:** Strengthening one pathway actively suppresses the competing pathway.

**Commitment:** Downstream computation learns to read from one pathway first and later ignores the other, even if the other remains available.

**Pathway loss:** The unused pathway loses its information because training no longer preserves it.

**Mixed mechanism:** Commitment and suppression both contribute.

Develop predictions that distinguish them.

## B2. Run a release-time experiment

Instead of masking a pathway only for the first 600 steps, use a predeclared range such as:

- 100 steps.
- 250 steps.
- 400 steps.
- 600 steps.

Apply the comparison to both early-readout and late-readout pathway restrictions.

Use at least one pretrained parent and training configuration in which both directions have previously produced competent final models.

Prioritize the highest-information contrasts rather than spending the entire budget on every combination.

Track the model beyond mask release.

If a masked model does not learn the task adequately, report it as behaviorally non-competent rather than evidence of a new solution.

## B3. Determine what happens to the unused route

Measure whether the unused pathway:

1. Still contains useful source information.
2. Still transmits that information.
3. Is ignored by downstream components.
4. Has been functionally suppressed.
5. Can be reactivated without complete retraining.

Test the pathway after mask release through controlled causal interventions.

For example, if an early-readout pathway remains informative but the model stops using it, that would support commitment by downstream readers rather than loss of information.

If strengthening one pathway causally reduces the other's information or computation even when downstream readout is controlled, that would support genuine competition.

## B4. Test reversibility and critical periods

Determine whether the selected implementation can be redirected after it has emerged.

Possible experiments:

- Early versus late masking.
- Temporary unfreezing of the unused route.
- Restoring pretrained pathway weights.
- Short targeted retraining of the previously unused downstream reader.

Do not confuse temporary output disruption with a genuine change of learned algorithm.

Measure whether any changed organization persists and remains competent after intervention removal.

## B5. Improve the learning-transition analysis

The step-546 predictor failed near the boundary.

Construct and test a more defensible checkpoint-level predictor only if mechanistic measurements justify it.

Evaluate it prospectively on runs not used to select the predictor.

Compare against basic baselines such as current loss, accuracy, and existing readout measures.

If no predictor works, preserve that negative result.

**Required scientific answer:** Is training selecting between actively competing pathways, committing to one available route, losing unused information, or combining these mechanisms?

- Attention scores.
- Attention weights.
- Values.
- Output projections.
- MLP2.
- Candidate readout.

Test the hypothesis that the adapted earlier layers place relational information into a representation that changes the frozen mixture heads' reliability weighting.

Distinguish between:

- Encoding a new source-reliability estimate.

- Altering the candidate likelihood representation.
- Reusing a pre-existing decision algorithm.

**Required scientific answer:** What does the learned repair representation encode, where is it causally used, and which aspects of its necessity and uniqueness can be established?
