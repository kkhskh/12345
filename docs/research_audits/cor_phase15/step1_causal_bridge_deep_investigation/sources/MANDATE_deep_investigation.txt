## 2. Correct the three concrete errors from the last submission

These corrections are prerequisites, not the main research.

**A. Same-cut theorem.** A downstream output is invariant to a zero-sum change in multiple writers only if all causal paths from those edited writers to the downstream output pass through the held-fixed aggregate. Explicitly exclude bypasses. For example, with \(S=c_1+c_2\) and \(Y=S+c_1\), holding \(S\) fixed does not hold \(Y\) fixed. Correct the theorem and its proof.

**B. Per-head identifiability.** Two heads with identical Q/K/V computations can have output matrices transformed as \(W_O^1\mapsto W_O^1+E\) and \(W_O^2\mapsto W_O^2-E\). The total computation is unchanged, while their individual readout contributions change. Therefore per-head measurements in one frozen checkpoint are not automatically uniquely identifiable explanations of the computed function. Formally distinguish fixed-parameterization observability, causal intervention attribution, and identifiability modulo function-preserving reparameterizations.

**C. Mechanism coverage.** The last research package concentrated on softmax-weighted contributions and final LayerNorm while explicitly leaving agreement-sensitive nonlinear MLPs, Q/K-mediated relations, and deeper rereading circuits unexamined. These are major omissions. They must now be investigated, not relegated to another open-questions document.

Do not spend most of the task polishing these corrections.

## 3. Reproduce the independently discovered agreement-sensitive Transformer circuit

This is your initial positive control. You must reproduce and understand it before attempting broader claims.

Construct a one-layer, three-token, four-dimensional pre-norm Transformer-like block.

Two source tokens independently carry evidence signs:

\[
a,b\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

Use two orthonormal mean-zero residual directions \(e_1,e_2\) to carry these signals. A read token initially has no candidate-specific logit contribution.

Use a causal attention head with uniform attention, initially implemented by \(W_Q=W_K=0\), and choose the value/output map so the read token receives:

\[
a e_1+b e_2.
\]

Use a standard nonlinear GELU MLP with four hidden units and preactivations:

\[
a+b,\quad -(a+b),\quad a-b,\quad -(a-b).
\]

Construct a third readout direction \(e_3\) and an MLP output proportional to:

\[
q(a,b)
=
\operatorname{GELU}(a+b)
+\operatorname{GELU}(-a-b)
-\operatorname{GELU}(a-b)
-\operatorname{GELU}(-a+b).
\]

For binary evidence, \(q>0\) when the signs agree and \(q<0\) when they disagree.

Read the difference between two candidate logits along \(e_3\), optionally after an explicitly included final LayerNorm.

Required outputs:

- Exact parameter matrices.
- Forward computation for all four \((a,b)\) combinations.
- Correct candidate-margin signs.
- Independent interventions on source A and source B.
- Causal readout decomposition before and after the MLP.
- Confirmation that fixed attention weights are not responsible for the sign change.
- An analytic explanation of how the MLP implements the agreement-sensitive operation.
- A separate implementation that reproduces the behavior.

This establishes that a Transformer-like computation **can implement an agreement-dependent decision rule**. It does not establish that trained Transformers use such a rule, that the mechanism is novel, or that the original COR obstruction score causes anything.

Do not overclaim.

But do not dismiss this positive control as irrelevant merely because its weights were constructed deliberately. It demonstrates architectural possibility and provides a concrete mechanism to generalize, perturb, and attack.

## 6. For each mechanism, perform an actual causal experiment in the synthetic system

The experiments must be designed before looking at favorable outcomes.

At minimum:

**Independent source interventions.** Change A while holding B fixed, change B while holding A fixed, and change both.

**Agreement-changing interventions.** Produce transitions from agreement to disagreement and vice versa.

**Strength controls.** Distinguish changing the relationship from merely increasing or decreasing evidence magnitude.

**Readout controls.** Check whether the measured response concerns the intended candidate margin.

**Mechanism ablations.** Disable or remove the alleged comparing/gating/reconciliation computation and test whether the specific relation-sensitive effect disappears.

**Matched-state controls.** Compare interventions that preserve selected aggregate statistics while changing proposed source relationships, wherever such pairs are computationally possible.

**Negative controls.** Construct systems that aggregate evidence but do not compute the hypothesized relation.

**Reparameterization controls.** Check whether the proposed explanatory quantity survives relevant function-preserving transformations.

Do not claim that you have independently intervened on a consistency score if that score is a deterministic function of the underlying evidence and no unique intervention on the score exists.

The interventions are on actual model variables.

The relation is a specified property of those variables.

You must establish whether the downstream computation is sensitive to that property.

One crucial check: if all information from the sources passes through an additive residual sum \(S\), then downstream output is determined by \(S\). A purported consistency effect that survives while \(S\) and all other downstream inputs are fixed is impossible in a no-bypass model. Use this as a screening theorem, not as an excuse to dismiss relational computations where \(S\) retains the necessary information.

## 8. Attempt the actual noncircular causal-bridge theorem

This is the central mathematical deliverable.

For the strongest mechanism that survives investigation, define:

- A frozen computational model class \(\mathcal F\).
- A candidate-margin function \(m_c\).
- Independently identified source representations \(z_1,z_2,\ldots\).
- A consistency-related relationship \(C_c(z_1,z_2,\ldots)\).
- A structural computational operator \(G\) that actually processes those variables.
- An intervention family \(\mathcal I\).
- Explicit assumptions \(\mathcal A\) that can be checked without using the target response.

Then prove the strongest defensible implication:

\[
\mathcal A
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal P(C_c,\Delta m_c).
\]

Here \(\mathcal P\) must be a specific statement about candidate-decision response, not an unspecified association.

Possible outcomes include:

- A signed directional effect under a restricted intervention.
- A candidate-ranking implication under an explicitly identified aggregation rule.
- A conditional response interaction with a specified sign.
- A mechanism-specific invariance or reversal rule.
- A causal sufficiency result that constrains the candidate margin.

Do not assume one of these must hold.

If a candidate theorem fails, build the counterexample and derive the missing condition.

Then ask whether that missing condition is:

1. Independently identified.
2. Computationally meaningful.
3. Plausibly present in a Transformer.
4. Noncircular.
5. Not merely the desired output sign in disguise.

If it fails these checks, reject the theorem as an explanatory bridge.

If the only successful theorem is that a deliberately programmed agreement gate produces an agreement-dependent output, call it an **implemented-mechanism existence theorem**, not an independently discovered decision theory.

## 13. Distinguish mathematical possibility from actual trained-model discovery

**Existence:** A Transformer-like block can implement a decision rule sensitive to independently specified source agreement.

**Identification:** The defining computational structure can be recognized independently from model weights, representations, and allowed observations.

**Scientific distinctiveness:** The consistency-based account adds something beyond established mechanisms.

**Empirical validation:** A trained Transformer actually exhibits the identified mechanism under prospective intervention.
