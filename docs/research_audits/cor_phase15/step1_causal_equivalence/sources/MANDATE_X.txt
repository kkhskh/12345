## 3. CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTION

**Do independently trained Transformers implement the same abstract relational computation through different internal causal mechanisms, or do they learn genuinely different algorithms that produce similar candidate decisions?**

This question is the starting point, not a predetermined conclusion.

Investigate four possibilities:

**H1 — Shared abstract computation**

Different models implement a common relational variable or operation, such as eligible-group count \(S\), per-group eligibility \(m_g\), or a mathematically equivalent representation.

**H2 — Distributed equivalence**

The same high-level computation exists, but it is distributed across different tokens, heads, layers, or pathways and cannot be captured by one localized mediator.

**H3 — Different algorithms**

Models achieve similar decisions using genuinely different computations, distinguishable through appropriate causal interventions or distribution shifts.

**H4 — Partial equivalence or identification failure**

Models may share some computations but not others, or the available interventions may be insufficient to determine equivalence.

Do not assume a common variable must exist.

Equally, do not declare algorithms different merely because one representation-alignment method fails.

The objective is to determine what the evidence supports.
