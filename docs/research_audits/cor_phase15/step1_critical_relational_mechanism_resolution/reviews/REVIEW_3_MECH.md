# XIII-C Review 3: mechanism and causality (independent, adversarial AI-agent review; not human peer review)

**Scope.** Mandate §§3–8 and 13. I read:

- `logs/NOTES_DEV.md`, `scripts/predictions_13c.json`, `logs/score_13c.json`, `logs/uncertainty_13c.json`, `logs/analyze_A_final.json`;
- the scripts named in the brief;
- the raw `*_conf`, `*_dev` and `adapt3_*` outputs, and the checkpoint `curve.json` loss histories.

**What I ran.** Only read-only Python on the JSON logs. Nothing was trained, and no existing file was modified. Every number below that is

## Summary verdict

The frozen verdicts stand. Several of the mechanistic readings the authors want to draw from them, however, are stronger than the
experiments justify.

**What is solid:**
- The fresh initialisation of the readout side, not the data stream, decides whether a q-mass mixture head forms.
- Blocking one pathway during the first 600 steps can change which organisation is reached, and the change persists after the block is
  lifted.
- The final-layer two-colluder repair fails robustly. L2 is still at 0.00 with 3× the steps and with 3× the learning rate.
- The DAS-vs-probe dissociation (R5) is real as an empirical fact.
- The parallel-route decomposition (R8) is internally consistent.

**What is not established:**
- **(a) "The readout map (WA) carries the init effect."** In both split cells WA and data change together, and the result is a post-hoc
  reading of a *falsified* prediction.
- **(b) "Pathway competition" as the mechanism of seed selection.** One of the two l2q arms is an under-trained model, R2c failed twice,
  and the interventions show path dependence rather than a competition that has been shown to happen in unintervened runs.
- **(c) R3 as "routing changes the decision law".** ρ_KL tracks distance from Bayes almost perfectly (r = −0.93 with log KL). R3(b) fails
  under the lapse-fitted family that the authors themselves recommend reporting.
- **(d) R4's "per-group format accessibility".** supG fails at the frozen budget and succeeds only with more optimisation. The decisive
  comparator, supL2 at the longer budget, was never run.
- **(e) R7 as identification of a mediator.** At ALL2 the E-vs-A margins are the model's *behavioural* margins, reproduced to within
  0.01–0.03.

## Must-fix

**M1. R1b does not identify WA. The claim must be labelled post-hoc and needs a discriminating run.**

The split cells are (L, W, D) = (a, b, b) and (b, a, b). In uP163:
- L22/W21/D21 → (0.45, 0.84), against the s21 reference (0.38, 0.91);
- L21/W22/D22 → (0.82, 0.16), against the s22 reference (0.82, 0.25).

So, relative to the reference it lands on, each split cell differs only in L. Relative to the L2-init reference, it differs in W *and* D.
Combined with R1a (f(a,a,b) ≈ f(a,a,a)), the data fit "outcome = g(W)". They fit several alternatives equally well:
1. a W × D interaction, for example: what matters is the alignment of WA's initial readout directions with the first batches. R1a then
   excludes D only when W = L, and only under additivity;
2. outcome set by whichever seed is shared by two of the three factors. Unprincipled, but not excluded by these four cell types;
3. joint L + W dependence in which L matters only when W does not oppose it.

The single discriminating run is **uP163 L21/W22/D21**: W differs from both L and data. "WA carries it" predicts early readout (≈ s22).
Alternatives 1–2 predict a hybrid or mixture (≈ s21). The mirror run is L22/W21/D22. Two runs complete the 2³ design in one parent.

Until those runs exist:
- R1 must be reported as FALSIFIED as frozen. It predicted that L2 carries the effect.
- "WA carries it" must be presented as an exploratory hypothesis generated by that falsification, with n = 2 cells in one parent.

A cheap, no-training complement: the step-0 predictor search (`init_features.py`) has only layer-2 features (head masses, the
layer-2 / layer-0 gradient ratio). All 17 development fine-tunes have L = W, so that search could not separate L from W at all. Add WA
features to it, for example the alignment of the initial WA with the readout-token resid1 likelihood direction.

**M2. The "data effect ≤ 0.02, interaction ≤ 0.02" statements are development-only. Confirmation contradicts them for the continuous
residue.**

`analyze_A_final.json` gives, for the confirmation 2 × 2s:

| parent | quantity | data effect | interaction | init effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| uP161 (20 vs 23) | lik_RO1 | −0.09 | +0.18 | −0.33 |
| uP161 (20 vs 23) | ρ | | −0.15 | |
| uP163 | lik_RO1 | 0.06 | 0.11 | |

In uP161 the crossed cell L23/D20 has lik 0.24, between its init diagonal (0.42) and its data diagonal (0.002). The arm passed R1a only by
0.20 vs 0.28, which is borderline under the pre-declared ±0.05 rule.

The defensible reading is narrower:
- the *binary* event (whether a head forms) is init-determined in every contrast;
- the *continuous residue* in hybrids (how much early readout survives) depends on the data stream and on init × data.

CAUSES §A2/A4, the H-A2/H-A3 rows and D7 ("route_Q follows the initialisation") must be updated. Note also that route_Q in the development
early sibling differs by 0.14 between the two data streams (0.08 vs 0.22), which is a larger difference than the 0.37 vs 0.39 quoted as
equivalence.

**M3. The uP161 21/21/21 l2q arm is an under-trained model and should not support "competition" or R3.**

Evidence (`curve.json`, `unit_metrics_conf.jsonl`, `fair_fits_conf.json`):
- loss plateaus at about 0.85 during the mask, against about 0.57–0.65 for the other masked runs at step 600, and ends at 0.644, the
  highest final loss of the masked runs (0.52–0.57 for the others);
- at step 546: slope_e 0.002, acc 0.60, so no relational learning at all;
- at step 4000: KL 0.12–0.21, acc 0.79, slope_e 0.55, slope_W 0.53;
- the PM fit explains only half the KL (PM KL 0.097 of 0.207);
- the PML fit is degenerate (λ = −4.1, which is not a valid lapse).

lik_RO1 ≥ 0.4 is the *default* state of every run early in training (0.12–0.6 by steps 111–546). A run that fails to learn the relation
therefore passes R2b without having adopted the early-readout *solution*. Required changes:
- report this arm as behaviourally non-competent;
- state that R2b rests on a single competent run (uP160 23/23/23 l2q: KL 0.004, slope_e 0.91);
- exclude the arm's −0.37 ρ shift from any interpretation of R3.

(Dropping it actually raises the R3(b) correlation: −0.92, n = 11. The problem is interpretation, not the verdict.)

**M4. R3 is confounded with distance from Bayes, and fails under PML.**

Over the 11 competent units:

| correlation | r |
|---|---|
| ρ_KL vs log KL(model ‖ Bayes) | −0.93 |
| lik_RO1 vs log KL | +0.91 |
| lik_RO1 vs ρ_KL, partial on log KL | −0.49 |

ρ < 1 is only identified by deviation from Bayes, and T2 shows that ρ is equivalent to a misstated q-reliability. "Early-readout models
are less accurate, and their residual error has a tempering shape" explains R3 as well as "the routing changes the decision law".

Under PML (lapse fitted jointly; DECISION_LAW says "Report PML"):
- the uP160 l2q shift is −0.07, below min_shift 0.08;
- corr(lik_RO1, ρ_PML) is −0.64 over n = 12, above the −0.7 threshold.

R3 must be reported with these sensitivities. Its reading should be limited to "intervention-induced organisation changes move ρ_KL with
accuracy, in the predicted direction". It should not be stated as a separable decision-law effect.

**M5. R4's format claim rests on a comparator that was never run at the budget where supG works.**

The facts:
- In confirmation, supG = 0.28 at 1000 steps (FAILED). The exploratory runs give 0.90 at 3000 steps and 0.93 at lr 3e-3. L2 stays at
  0.00 in both.
- That robustly shows that L2's failure is not an optimisation-budget artefact.
- The *format* claim, however, is the supG-vs-supL2 contrast, and supL2 exists only at 1000 steps / lr 1e-3. At that budget supG also
  failed in the confirmation unit.

supL2 is not architecturally excluded:
- m is added at the readout token, so the query side can carry it;
- the layer-2 logit score_g = (E m)ᵀ W_Qᵀ W_K x_g can be linear in m_{H(−g)}, provided the keys encode group identity;
- so the needed per-group gating is representable.

The supL2 failure is therefore a learnability or speed result, the same kind of result as the supG failure at 1000 steps.

Required changes:
- run supL2 (and ideally L1only) at 3000 steps and at lr 3e-3, before claiming "format";
- otherwise, restate R4 as "information present group-agnostically is learned far more slowly than per-group information; whether it is
  usable at all is open";
- delete the sentence in JOINT §C5 "the strongest single-optimisation alternative is excluded, because supG and L2 use the same optimiser".
  Confirmation refutes it: supG's success is budget-dependent.

Two further points:
- supG hands layer 2 exactly the Corollary-P quantity, m_{H(−g)} at g's tokens. Its success is close to "supplying the gating input".
  It should not be read as evidence about what the early layers construct. R5 shows the construction is *not* that linear quantity.
- The early-unit L2 value of 0.38 (threshold 0.4; uP161 s22 0.07; XIII-B 0.53 and 0.63) is a near-miss. The C6 conclusion "not a class
  property" fits it; do not call it support.

**M6. R7 at ALL2 identifies a sufficient subspace, not an E-specific mediator.**

Behaviour alone (no patching, `d_das_conf.json` "behaviour") gives, for uP163 22/22/21:
- E5 R²_E 0.94, R²_A2 0.70 (margin 0.23);
- E3 0.74 / 0.003;
- QF4 0.97 / 0.24.

The k = 8 DAS patch gives E5 0.89 / 0.67 (margin 0.22), E3 0.58 / 0.004 and QF4 0.94 / 0.26. The E-over-A margins that make up R7(b, c)
are the model's *own* behavioural margins, inherited by any subspace with a high own-share. They say nothing about the representation. Only
two quantities carry representational content: own-share relative to random subspaces, and the excess over SRCFREE.

The site makes the identification weaker still:
- ALL2 contains every token, including the edited source tokens;
- at ALL2, FULLSITE is trivially 1.0;
- for QF5/QF4, and for the edited q′ tokens in E5/E3, the residual at those tokens contains the edit itself (the value embeddings);
- so a shared 8-dim subspace can partly re-inject the input edit, for example as value identity or agreement codes, and let the
  unpatched layer 2 recompute.

The source-specific part is modest. SRCFREE already gives own-share 0.51 / −0.03 / 0.70 / 0.66 on E5 / E3 / QF5 / QF4, so the excess is
0.37 / 0.60 / 0.23 / 0.21.

Required changes:
- restate R7 as "an 8-dim subspace of the full layer-2 input suffices to transmit the model's own eligibility response, with a
  source-specific excess of about 0.2–0.6 over a base-rate patch";
- drop "E-specific mediator";
- or re-run DAS with the edited tokens excluded from the site, and report the patch-minus-behaviour margin, not the raw margin.

## Should-fix

**S1. "Pathway competition" vs path dependence or lock-in (R2).**

The two competent interventions (ro0 on uP160 s21; l2q on uP160 s23), plus the development ro0, show:
- blocking route X for 600 steps leads to adoption of route Y;
- Y persists after release.

There is no loss spike at release (ro0: 0.591 → 0.511 at 600 → 700), so off-distribution damage is unlikely for these three runs.

What is *not* shown is that competition operates in the unintervened seed selection:
- R2c, the frozen test of the claim that the race is decided early, failed in 2 of 5 diagnostic units;
- uP163 22/21/21 had mix546 = 0.26 yet formed a head later (final 0.84, lik 0.45);
- the "decline of early readout after head formation" is correlational.

The interventions are consistent with an alternative: downstream readers commit to whichever source is informative first, and the other
channel goes unused. That is "readers lock in", with no active suppression.

Suggested discriminating analyses:
- release-time sweeps (T = 100, 250, 400) to find a critical period;
- for ro0, check after release whether layer-0 readout→source attention returns to its pretrained pattern. If it does, while lik_RO1 stays
  ≈ 0, the readers ignore present information, which supports lock-in by readers rather than loss of the feature.

Wording should be "early commitment / path dependence, biased by initialisation". "The early race decides" overstates, given R2c.

**S2. R5: the probe nulls may be a filter-vs-pattern artefact; DAS sufficiency is not matched by a necessity test.**

How the probe directions are built:
- they are ridge weights (λ = 1 on standardised features, n = 3000, so near-OLS), mapped back as W/sd;
- these are *filter* (decoder) directions;
- with correlated features, a filter can be nearly orthogonal to the direction along which the variable is actually encoded (the activation
  pattern Σ_x w; the standard filter-vs-pattern distinction);
- interchange along the filter changes what the probe reads, but not necessarily what the network reads.

The m probes were also fitted on *mean-pooled* QS/OS features but applied per token, whereas Corollary P implies a group-specific per-token
code. The responsibility probe is per-token, which partly answers this, but it is still a filter.

Missing controls (all cheap, no training):
- interchange along pattern directions (Σ_x w, or class-conditional mean differences);
- decode m and r_g from the 3-dim DAS projection;
- mean-ablate the DAS subspace on base and donor. This necessity test is missing; the probes received both tests, DAS only sufficiency;
- evaluate the DAS patch on J_D0 and DECOY donors, where the exact change is 0. DAS was trained and tested only on J_DM pairs from one
  distribution (first half / second half), so it was never checked that it does *not* transmit m-irrelevant differences.

Without these, the reading should be "the causally sufficient low-dimensional subspace is not aligned with the probes' decoder
directions". C3 items 4–5 ("a distributed code whose causally used part lies in directions that linear decoders do not find";
"decodability ≠ use") overreach.

A further mismatch: DAS uses the QS + RO site (ceiling 0.93), while the probes were tested one site at a time (ceilings 0.30–0.43). The
nulls (≤ 0.015) are far below those ceilings, so this does not reverse the finding, but the comparison should be made at matched sites.

**S3. R8 additivity is largely a linearity test.**

The routes are a *partition of token positions* of the layer-1 attention output. For small edits in a near-linear regime, the sufficiencies
of any partition sum to about 1. Redundancy ≈ 0 and necessity ≈ sufficiency are therefore expected whether there are "separate
mechanisms" or one distributed mechanism read at several positions.

The informative content of R8 is the *split*: Q-heavy in mixture units, R/O-heavy in early units. Phrases such as "genuinely separate
routes" and "no backup pathway" should be softened, or tested with large edits, or with route cuts applied during training.

**S4. The D1 SHUFD decoy and the R²-over-slope metric.**

By construction, SHUFD's change is driven by *another pair's* relational content. Its R² against *this* pair's Bayes change is ≈ 0 for any
mechanism in which the rows matter at all. SHUFD therefore tests pair-specificity of the row content, which TRUE vs shuffled establishes
trivially. It does not test mechanism specificity.

It is also only partly displacement-matched: L1 displacement is 0.27–0.28, against 0.39–0.42 for TRUE.

A more informative report is SHUFD's R² against the *permuted* pair's Bayes change (dB[perm_d]). If the rows carry Bayes-shaped content
generically, that R² should be high, which would *strengthen* the row-mediator claim.

The choice of R² over slope is sound and well argued. Large non-specific perturbations do produce positive slopes. Keep both columns.

**S5. R6 and the no-op claim in the text.**

R6 is FALSIFIED as frozen. The only failing criterion is NOOP = 7.2e-7 rather than 0, and it is a float-order difference: `run_rows`
recomputes layer 2 by hand, while the reference is `F.forward`. Accept the frozen verdict; do not re-score.

CAUSAL_CONTROLS §D2 currently says "max |Δlogit| = 0.0 in every unit tested (exact, not within tolerance)". That is now false for the
d_rows no-op in confirmation and must be corrected.

Only one unit was diagnostic for R6, so even the passing criteria are n = 1.

**S6. Statistical units and equivalence language.**

- Training is deterministic, so run-to-run noise cannot be estimated by repetition. The only noise estimates are episode bootstraps and the
  stream sd of route_Q (0.03). Those measure the *measurement* error, not the sensitivity of the training outcome to small perturbations.
- "Follows the initialisation" claims built on differences of 0.02–0.05 (route_Q 0.37 vs 0.39, ρ 0.85 vs 0.87) therefore have no
  reference for what difference would count as meaningful.
- Cheap fix: a tiny-perturbation replicate, such as D′ = D with one batch resampled, or a 1e-6 perturbation of the WA init.
- Every confirmation prediction is n = 1–2 fine-tunes of 1–3 parents. R4, R5, R6 and R7 are each a single unit.
- Verdicts should be written as "confirmed in this unit", and nothing should be generalised to "the early class" or "repairs" without
  that qualifier.

**S7. R2c thresholds sit in development "gaps" chosen from 7 runs.**

The two failures are exactly the hybrid-trajectory cases: late head formation, and a head at 0.72 with residue 0.24. This is evidence that
mix546 does not decide the outcome in hybrids. It should be stated as such, and not as threshold bad luck.

**S8. ro0 and l2q mask the very quantity each outcome is scored on.**

- ro0 masks layer-0 readout→source attention, the substrate that lik_RO1 measures.
- l2q masks layer-2 readout→own-q attention, which is mix_mass.
- The persistence after release is the real result, and it should be framed that way.
- The R2a/R2b thresholds would be met trivially if release did not matter. A short "release at step 600, measure at 700" checkpoint would
  show how much of the outcome was already fixed at release.

## Interpretations that survive

1. **The initialisation of the fresh head (L + W jointly) determines the binary organisation event**, whether a q-mass mixture head forms.
   This holds in 4 of 4 parent-level 2 × 2s: init effect on mix_mass 0.70–0.80 in three of them, and both diagonals formed heads in the
   fourth (uP161). The data stream has no effect on that event.
2. **Within (L, W), layer-2 init alone has little effect when W and D are held fixed** (R1b): the split cells sit within 0.10 of the
   reference they share W and D with. Whether that is W or W × D is open (M1).
3. **Path dependence.** Blocking one pathway for 600 steps redirects a competent run to the other organisation, and the redirection
   persists. Shown in ro0 × 2 (development and confirmation) and l2q × 1 competent run.
4. **ρ_KL co-varies with organisation**, including under intervention, in the predicted direction (R3a passes on the two competent arms
   under PM). The mechanistic attribution is open (M4).
5. **The final-layer-only repair of the two-colluder case fails robustly.** Shown at 3× steps and 3× lr, with capacity matching (L2plus),
   and when m is supplied group-agnostically (at 1000 steps). One early block at layer 0 succeeds and one at layer 1 does not (n = 1
   unit; dev + conf).
6. **Decodability and causal use dissociate, at the level of decoder directions** (R5a/b). A 3-dim learned subspace at QS + RO is
   sufficient (0.80 held-out), while 3-dim interchange along the m / responsibility filters transfers ≤ 0.01. The frozen layer 2 uses the
   repaired input mainly through mixture-head attention rows (Shapley 0.82). The change in group mass correlates 0.84–0.89 with the change
   in exact role posterior.
7. **The relational effect splits additively across position-defined layer-1 routes**, with a class-dependent Q/R/O split (R8). Read this
   as a decomposition, not as proof of independent mechanisms (S3).
8. **The selected mixture heads' rows carry a Bayes-shaped change** (TRUE R² 0.97) that unrelated and shuffled rows do not reproduce
   (R6 specificity criteria all passed, n = 1).
9. **The XIII-B Q5a falsification was a site-coverage artefact** (early units carry eligibility at OS/RO, not QS). This is a solid
   development finding and is not affected by M6.

## Unresolved disagreements

- **Whether "WA carries the effect" can be stated at all before the L21/W22/D21 run.** My position: no, beyond "hypothesis consistent with
  2 cells". The authors may argue that W-only is the most parsimonious model. It is, but the falsified frozen prediction shows that their
  prior about which factor matters was wrong once already.
- **Whether R4 can be called "format accessibility".** My position: until supL2 is run at the longer budget, the evidence equally supports
  "per-group supply is learned faster". The authors' cross-arm argument (L2 fails at every budget) addresses L2, not supL2.
- **Whether ρ is a "decision law" or a deviation-from-Bayes index.** T2 already makes ρ indistinguishable from q-evidence tempering. The
  −0.93 correlation with log KL adds that, empirically in this population, it is close to a monotone function of accuracy. I would not
  call R3 evidence of a law-level causal effect of routing. The authors may reasonably reply that accuracy is itself downstream of
  routing; then the claim reduces to "routing affects accuracy", and should be stated that way.
- **Competition vs lock-in.** I consider the unintervened mechanism unresolved, given that R2c failed. A release-time sweep would settle
  much of it.
