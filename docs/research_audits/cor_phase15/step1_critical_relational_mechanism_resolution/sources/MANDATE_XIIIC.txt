# 1. NON-NEGOTIABLE SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

Investigation XIII-B established several important findings.

### Finding 1 — Different implementations can arise from the same pretrained parent

The original classification into early-readout and late-readout models is not fixed by pretraining.

Additional fine-tuning seeds caused:

- uP161 to move between late-readout, early-readout, and hybrid configurations.
- uP160 to move from early-readout to late-readout.
- uP163 and uP164 to change their readout organization.
- uP165 to remain comparatively stable across the tested seeds.

However, the fine-tuning seed changed both the initialization of fresh components and the training data stream.

**We do not know which cause produces the different solutions.**

This is a major unresolved causal question.

### Finding 2 — Solution organization correlates with the mathematical decision law

The fitted power-mean exponent, denoted ρ, is associated with the model's readout organization.

Reported ranges were approximately:

- Early-readout: ρ = 0.54–0.62.
- Late-readout: ρ = 0.86–0.98.
- Hybrid: intermediate values.

Across 29 fine-tunes, the readout signature and fitted ρ had a strong negative correlation of approximately −0.93.

But ρ is not uniquely identifiable as a distinct internal combination operator.

The same mathematical expression can be interpreted as transformed or tempered evidence followed by output rescaling.

A lapse floor also changes the fitted exponent.

Moreover, this parameter explains only a limited portion of the early-readout models' overall approximation error.

**We have not established why ρ differs, or what computational mechanism produces that difference.**

### Finding 3 — The mathematical sufficiency boundary is clearer than the neural implementation

For the specified three-group, two-colluder shared-copying generator:

- At exact copying, β = 1, on-support decisions can be computed from per-group self-agreement and the joint values at the target question.
- Additional cross-group comparisons at other questions are not required.
- At partial copying, such as β = 0.6, those cross-question relationships provide additional decision-relevant information.
- The restricted local-information decision falls approximately 3.6 percentage points below exact Bayes accuracy.

The adapted networks show different outcomes.

In late-readout models, adapting layers 0–1 while freezing layer 2 succeeds, whereas layer-2-only adaptation often fails to use the cross-question relationship.

In early-readout models, layer-2 adaptation can use more of that information.

But:

- The exact feature constructed by early-layer adaptation is unknown.
- Decodability does not prove causal use.
- The adaptation conditions were not matched for trainable capacity.
- Optimization failure has not been distinguished from an architectural accessibility constraint.
- The exact way the frozen final layer uses the new information remains unresolved.

### Finding 4 — Some causal controls remain defective or incomplete

The independent review of XIII-B found:

- The TOP decoy head was not genuinely matched for decision contribution.
- The MEAN control averaged rows across different target-question positions and was off-distribution.
- The alleged DAS no-op was actually an arbitrary subspace patch because QR decomposition of a tiny matrix still produces an orthonormal basis.
- The claimed independent restoration pathway was downstream on the same causal path.
- Balanced eligibility identification depended strongly on the chosen neural site.
- Some edits separated E from A1 but not from the threshold-4 alternative A2.
- Attention/MLP attribution was incomplete, especially in early-readout and hybrid models.
- Some causal predictions were sensitive to borderline thresholds and evaluation-stream noise.

**These must be addressed directly.**

Do not carry the defective controls forward unchanged.

# 2. CENTRAL SCIENTIFIC QUESTIONS

Investigation XIII-C must answer four questions.

**A. Learning causes**

What independently causes a model to develop an early-readout, late-readout, or hybrid implementation?

**B. Mathematical decision mechanisms**

Are the observed input-output differences caused by genuinely different evidence-combination computations, different evidence transformations, different calibration, or some combination of these?

**C. Information accessibility and repair**

What exact relational information is constructed or rerouted when a model adapts to two colluders, and why do different neural implementations have different repair capabilities?

**D. Causal identifiability**

Which identified components and relational variables genuinely mediate decisions under rigorous controls, and which previous explanations were partly artifacts of intervention design?

The research must investigate how these questions relate to one another.

For example:

Does a training configuration cause a particular routing organization, which in turn constrains the decision rule and the model's ability to adapt when the relationships between sources change?

This is a testable causal hypothesis, not an established fact.

# 3. WORKSTREAM A — CAUSALLY IDENTIFY WHY SOLUTION CLASSES CHANGE

**Highest priority. Mandatory controlled training experiment.**

Do not run additional seeds that change several factors simultaneously and then claim to have identified the cause.

## A1. Separate initialization from training-data order

Inspect `native_train.py` and the complete fine-tuning implementation.

Identify every use of the fine-tuning seed.

Separate at least:

1. Fresh layer-2 initialization.
2. Fresh readout initialization, where applicable.
3. Training-example generation and ordering.
4. Other stochastic behavior, if present.

Record all additional sources of randomness.

Modify the experimental harness so these factors can be controlled independently.

Preserve the existing architecture, generator, and target task unless the experiment explicitly manipulates them.

## A2. Run a factorial experiment

Use at least two distinct pretrained parents representing different previously observed solution organizations.

At minimum, use a controlled two-by-two comparison of:

- Two fresh-component initialization conditions.
- Two independent training-data-order conditions.

Run every combination within each selected pretrained parent.

This gives at least eight paired fine-tuning runs.

Reuse identical initialization tensors when changing only data order.

Reuse identical training streams when changing only initialization.

Where feasible, separate layer-2 initialization from readout initialization through an additional targeted comparison.

If computational limits prevent a larger factorial design, complete the core design before spending compute on additional exploratory models.

Do not quietly substitute uncontrolled seeds for the factorial experiment.

## A3. Measure the entire emergence process

Track over checkpoints:

- Early readout of candidate information.
- Routing of relational information into readout and source tokens.
- Layer-2 attention-row contributions.
- Layer-2 MLP contributions.
- Continuous readout organization.
- Candidate-decision accuracy.
- Fitted ρ.
- The transition from early heuristic behavior to the more correct rule.

Do not merely classify the final checkpoint.

Determine when each implementation begins to emerge and whether competing pathways develop simultaneously.

Investigate whether one pathway suppresses, replaces, or coexists with another.

## A4. Test competing causal explanations

At minimum:

**H-A1:** Fresh layer-2 initialization determines the computational organization.

**H-A2:** Training data order determines the organization.

**H-A3:** Initialization and data order interact.

**H-A4:** Early competition between readout and source-token routing pathways determines the organization.

**H-A5:** The apparent class is not a discrete choice but a continuous optimization outcome.

Use the factorial design to estimate main effects and interactions.

Where a clear early trajectory difference appears, perform an affordable intervention designed to change that trajectory.

Possible approaches include controlled freezing, delayed unfreezing, or selectively suppressing one competing route during early training.

Do not claim a causal training mechanism from correlations between final models alone.

## A5. Classify continuously

Retain the continuous readout and routing measurements.

Report early, late, and hybrid labels only as secondary descriptions.

Avoid claiming discrete basins merely because a histogram appears to have clusters.

Determine whether the results support:

- Distinct stable solutions.
- A continuous family.
- Mixed or overlapping solutions.
- Multiple attractors connected by intermediate states.

Account for uP164's previous threshold sensitivity.

**Required outcome:** An experimentally grounded explanation—or a precisely bounded failure to identify the cause—of why the same pretrained model can acquire different neural decision organizations.

# 4. WORKSTREAM B — RESOLVE THE MATHEMATICAL DECISION-LAW QUESTION

**Mandatory mathematical and empirical investigation.**

The strong correlation between readout organization and ρ is interesting but not a mechanistic explanation.

This workstream must determine what that parameter actually establishes.

## B1. Start with mathematical identifiability

Use the corrected nested power-mean formulation from XIII-B.

Explicitly derive the algebraic relationships between:

- Power-mean evidence combination.
- Tempered likelihoods.
- Output rescaling or sharpening.
- Reliability weighting.
- Calibration and lapse-floor variants.

Determine which descriptions are mathematically equivalent on the observed decision distribution.

**If two proposed algorithms are mathematically identical as input-output functions, no observational input-output experiment can distinguish them.**

Prove or explicitly demonstrate that non-identifiability.

Do not manufacture an empirical distinction between equivalent formulas.

Separate:

1. Mathematical equivalence.
2. Empirical differences between fitted decision families.
3. Neural implementation differences.
4. Causal differences under internal intervention.

## B2. Fit genuinely fair competing models

Compare the power-mean family against appropriately nested alternatives:

- Exact Bayesian mixture.
- Normalized product-style pooling.
- Tempered evidence models.
- Reliability reweighting.
- Output calibration.
- Lapse-floor and saturation variants.
- Other parsimonious alternatives justified by observed residuals.

Control model flexibility and parameter counts where possible.

Use multi-start fitting, explicit convergence checks, and held-out evaluation.

Report more than R².

Include:

- Held-out negative log-likelihood or an appropriate proper scoring rule.
- Calibration.
- Candidate-specific error.
- Residual patterns.
- Fit stability.
- Parameter uncertainty.

Do not use the earlier handicapped product-of-experts comparison.

Do not infer mechanism from tiny improvements in a flexible function fit.

## B3. Explain why the early class has ρ near 0.6

Test competing explanations:

- Architectural restriction.
- MLP capacity.
- Early readout pooling.
- Optimization bias.
- Evidence calibration.
- An interaction between routing and readout.
- A simpler approximation that happens to fit the training environment.

Use the controlled training results from Workstream A.

Run targeted architectural or causal interventions where feasible.

For example, restrict an early-readout pathway or alter a relevant MLP capacity while preserving the task and properly controlling other changes.

Determine whether changing the neural routing organization produces a predictable change in the fitted decision law.

A mere correlation between ρ and routing is insufficient.

## B4. Test whether ρ reflects a genuine computational difference

Examine candidate decisions under carefully constructed on-support relational and likelihood edits.

Where possible, use internal path interventions to determine whether the same effective mathematical weighting is implemented through different computations.

Do not claim a distinct internal operator when an equivalent evidence-tempering interpretation remains viable.

It is acceptable to conclude that the input-output law is distinguishable but the internal factorization is not.

## B5. Quantify the actual explanatory value

The earlier analysis found that ρ explained only about 15% of the early class's mismatch relative to the Bayesian mixture.

Determine where the rest of the approximation error comes from.

Investigate:

- Reliability estimation errors.
- Eligibility errors.
- Candidate-likelihood errors.
- Calibration.
- Routing limitations.
- Failure to combine information correctly.

Use causal and mathematical decomposition where identifiable.

Do not attribute all class-specific error to ρ.

## B6. Strengthen eligibility-variable discrimination

Previous edits distinguished E from A1, but not from A2, the threshold-4 alternative.

Construct on-support evaluation cases that discriminate E, A1, and A2 whenever the generator permits them.

If the relevant values cannot be separated under specific support constraints, state and prove those limitations.

Do not relabel an agreement-at-target-question response as full identification of E when other candidate rules remain observationally equivalent on the test set.

**Required outcome:** A defensible explanation of what the different decision laws mean, which mathematical interpretations are identifiable, and whether neural organization causally contributes to those differences.

# 5. WORKSTREAM C — IDENTIFY THE TWO-COLLUDER REPAIR COMPUTATION

**Mandatory. Principal connection between the mathematical foundation and neural mechanism.**

## C1. Preserve the correct distinction between exact and partial copying

Use the established settings:

- Three groups.
- Two colluding groups.
- Exact copying, β = 1.
- Partial copying, β = 0.6.

Respect the on-support sufficiency theorem for exact copying.

At β = 1, additional cross-question equality information is not required for the specified posterior.

At β = 0.6, cross-group relationships at other questions can be decision-relevant.

Reverify mathematical reference calculations and valid isolating episode pairs before new neural testing.

Use the true generator distribution and an exact or independently checked posterior.

## C2. Identify the feature built during early-layer adaptation

Investigation XIII-B showed that successful adaptation of layers 0–1 makes the decision respond to cross-question relational information.

But the precise constructed feature remains unknown.

Investigate candidate features including:

- Cross-group equality counts.
- The n4 statistic.
- Terms in the corrected joint-copying posterior.
- Joint-source reliability information.
- An alternative sufficient encoding.

Determine whether an adapted model constructs an explicit recognizable statistic or a distributed transformation.

Use probes only as an initial diagnostic.

Probing must not be mistaken for proof that the network uses that statistic.

## C3. Establish causal use, not just decodability

Create valid pairs that preserve:

- Per-group statistics.
- Candidate evidence at the target question.
- Other factors that should remain unchanged under the tested hypothesis.

Vary the cross-question relation.

Measure predicted and observed changes in candidate-specific decisions.

Trace the causal path from the other-question source tokens into the adapted representation and then into the final decision.

Use component and path interventions that distinguish alternative relational statistics.

Test whether removing the proposed information destroys the predicted decision change and whether appropriate restoration recovers it.

Compare against decoy features and source-independent patches.

## C4. Explain how a frozen final layer uses the repaired representation

In late-readout models, adapting layers 0–1 with layer 2 frozen can reach near-Bayesian performance.

Determine whether the adapted earlier layers:

- Change the eligibility weights seen by the frozen mixture.
- Alter candidate-likelihood representations.
- Supply a new joint-source feature.
- Encode the corrected posterior in a representation that the frozen layer already knows how to use.
- Exploit another pathway.

Do not claim that the final layer “reuses the same mixture algorithm” solely because the output is correct.

Test the frozen layer's causal response to the adapted features.

## C5. Fairly distinguish information access, optimization, and capacity

Previous comparisons used two trainable early blocks versus one trainable final block.

That is a capacity and optimization confound.

Design matched or carefully controlled experiments comparing:

- Layers 0–1 adaptation.
- Layer-2 adaptation.
- Fresh layer-2 adaptation.
- Selective attention or MLP adaptation.
- Small adapters at relevant readout or source-token positions.
- Explicitly supplied cross-question information to the otherwise frozen downstream computation.

Control or report:

- Trainable parameter count.
- Training examples.
- Optimization steps.
- Learning-rate searches.
- Initialization.
- Evaluation stream.
- Compute cost.

Exact equality of capacity may not be possible, but do not ignore the mismatch.

A failed layer-2 repair after four times the training steps is evidence against one simple optimization explanation, not proof of architectural impossibility.

Where useful, perform a direct information-access intervention: make a properly defined joint statistic available at the downstream input and test whether the frozen or retrained final layer can use it.

This may distinguish inaccessible information from inability to compute with accessible information.

## C6. Compare repairs across solution organizations

Use early-readout, late-readout, and hybrid models, including seeds that switched class.

Ask whether repair success tracks:

- Pretrained parent.
- Fine-tuning seed.
- Readout organization.
- Location of relational information.
- Fitted ρ.
- Another directly measured property.

The strongest result would show that changing the implementation changes the accessibility or use of the required information.

Avoid claiming this unless the experiments genuinely establish it.

**Required outcome:** Identify the neural operation that makes the two-colluder repair work, or sharply distinguish and eliminate the leading explanations with controlled negative results.

# 16. REQUIRED FINAL SCIENTIFIC ANSWERS

The research synthesis must answer the following questions explicitly.

**1.** Does fresh initialization, training-data order, or their interaction cause models to develop different decision-making implementations?

**2.** Is there an identifiable early event during training that explains which implementation develops?

**3.** Are the observed solution classes discrete, continuous, or a combination?

**4.** Why do early-readout models tend to have ρ near 0.6, while late-readout models have values closer to 1?

**5.** Which mathematical descriptions of those decision laws are genuinely distinguishable, and which are equivalent reparameterizations?

**6.** How much of the class-specific decision error is explained by evidence combination, calibration, reliability estimation, or other causes?

**7.** What exact relational information does successful early-layer adaptation construct in the partial-copying two-colluder environment?

**8.** Where is that information located, and what evidence establishes its causal use rather than simple decodability?

**9.** Why can an early-layer repair succeed when a final-layer repair fails? Is this information accessibility, optimization, trainable capacity, or another cause?

**10.** Does changing the learned routing organization change the model's ability to perform the more complicated decision?

**11.** Do attention-row mediation claims survive properly contribution-matched controls, valid no-ops, and distribution-aware interventions?

**12.** Is a variable-specific eligibility mediator identifiable in early-readout models at the correct neural site?

**13.** Can the experiments distinguish E from A1 and A2, rather than only ruling out one alternative?

**14.** Do genuinely separate causal routes exist, or have previous restorations only demonstrated serial transmission?

**15.** What explains the decision effect outside the dominant attention pathway, especially in hybrid and early-readout models?

**16.** Which internal changes, if any, predict the transition to more correct decisions?

**17.** What mathematical principles survive across different implementations, and which conclusions remain specific to this source generator?

**18.** Which findings strengthen the broader COR foundations, which previous interpretations must be revised, and which critical questions remain open?

Do not answer a question confidently if the evidence is inadequate.

Distinguish mathematical proof, controlled causal evidence, independent confirmation, exploratory findings, and speculation.

# 18. FINAL DIRECTIVE

Investigation XIII-B produced an important discovery:

**The same pretrained model can develop different internal ways of making relational decisions, and those ways are associated with different mathematical decision behavior and different abilities to use information under changed source relationships.**

But we do not yet understand why those implementations arise, whether the mathematical differences represent fundamentally different computations, or exactly how new relational information is incorporated during successful adaptation.

Several controls used to defend the causal explanation were also imperfect.

These are not small remaining details.

**They are central to whether COR can claim to understand the causal structure of these learned relational decisions.**

Therefore:

- Separate initialization and data-order effects through controlled experiments.
- Investigate the training process that produces the different internal organizations.
- Resolve the mathematical identifiability of the decision-law differences.
- Explain the remaining class-specific decision errors.
- Identify and causally test the information used by the successful two-colluder repairs.
- Fairly distinguish information accessibility from trainable capacity and optimization.
- Correct the defective causal controls.
- Investigate the weakly identified early-readout mediator.
- Distinguish E, A1, and A2 where mathematically possible.
- Account for remaining attention/MLP pathways.
- Preserve negative findings and falsifications.
- Integrate the results into one coherent computational and mathematical explanation.

**Do not stop at correlation. Do not stop at decodability. Do not stop at successful adaptation. Do not stop at passing frozen predictions.**
