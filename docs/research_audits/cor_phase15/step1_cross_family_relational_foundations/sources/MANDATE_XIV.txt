# 1. THE SCIENTIFIC STARTING POINT

Investigations IX–XIII-E have established or suggested several distinctions:

- A model may represent useful relational information without using it correctly.
- Different training runs may reach similar decisions through different neural pathways.
- The relational information sufficient for correct decisions depends on the source-generating process.
- Changing the accessibility of information can change what a downstream computation learns.
- Short pathway interventions can alter the organization ultimately learned by a model.
- Neural activation subspaces can successfully transfer decision effects without uniquely identifying a natural internal variable.
- Certain causal-intervention metrics previously used in COR were non-diagnostic, as demonstrated by ideal-mechanism reference calculations.

These claims do not all have equal evidential status.

Some are mathematical results under restricted assumptions.

Some have substantial causal evidence in T4.

Some remain weak, model-specific, or unidentified.

Some previous interpretations have been falsified or withdrawn.

Investigation XIV must preserve those distinctions.

## Important corrections from XIII-E

1. The claim that the readout-map initialization uniquely determines the learned implementation was falsified in XIII-D.

2. XIII-E found a pooled, within-parent readout-draw effect, but no explanatory initialization statistic. The result is driven partly by graded variation in one parent, and cross-parent generality is unestablished.

3. Blocking a route for 100 steps changed the learned organization in 6/6 tested conditions.

4. Longer early blocks generally produced larger changes than shorter blocks.

5. Late blocks also altered organization. The earlier theory of a critical period that closes was not supported.

6. The off-route sham was not performed.

7. The noise control was applied at a different neural location and was not a fully satisfactory comparator.

8. Early and late interventions differed in persistence, but the strongest evidence was exploratory and relied on only a few runs.

9. Several causal metrics failed ideal-reference checks and are now considered non-diagnostic.

10. The proposed natural three-dimensional relational carrier was reinterpreted: one or two stable directions sufficed in the tested models, and successful subspaces were not unique.

11. The best-supported causal findings concern relational signal transfer, reverse-swap sensitivity, and specific attention-row effects under properly interpreted interventions.

12. The original obstruction/discrepancy framework has not been established.

Do not restore stronger versions of these claims without new evidence.

## I-A. Correct the missing off-route sham and location-matched noise controls

XIII-E's masking result is promising, but its interpretation still depends on inadequate controls.

Design three matched conditions:

**Condition 1: True route intervention**

Block the relevant early-readout or mixture pathway using the established XIII-E intervention.

**Condition 2: Off-route sham**

Apply the corresponding operation to an edge or pathway that does not belong to either proposed causal route.

Ensure that the sham matches the real intervention's technical procedure as closely as possible.

**Condition 3: Location-matched disturbance**

Introduce a disturbance at the same relevant neural location as the real intervention, matched for a prespecified measure of perturbation severity.

Do not compare a real route block at one site against unrelated noise at another site and call the comparison controlled.

### Requirements

- Use both established model organizations.
- Use at least three paired data-order seeds per principal condition if feasible.
- Use identical initialization tensors across matched conditions.
- Retain the 100-step intervention as the primary comparison.
- Include ordinary no-intervention baselines.
- Verify that the sham does not unintentionally interrupt either proposed route.
- Predefine how the disturbance magnitude is matched.
- Measure training competence, not only organization.
- Report continuous pathway measurements, not just early/mixture labels.

Evaluate the final model and the persistence of the intervention effect.

### Scientific question

**Does blocking a particular information pathway alter the learned organization more reliably than a comparable intervention that does not block that pathway?**

If both interventions produce similar changes, weaken the route-specific interpretation.

If only the genuine route intervention produces the predicted change, strengthen the route-specific causal conclusion, while retaining other plausible explanations.

A failed sham design cannot be treated as a successful control.

---

## I-B. Test whether early interventions produce more persistent changes

XIII-E found that late interventions can also change a model's organization.

The interesting remaining hypothesis is not that learning becomes completely insensitive later.

It is that **changes induced earlier may be more persistent than equally strong changes induced later**.

This must be tested correctly.

### Experimental design

Compare early and late interventions using:

- The same intervention duration.
- Comparable intervention severity.
- The same two baseline model organizations.
- Paired data-order seeds.
- Identical starting initializations within each paired comparison.
- Identical measurements of the resulting organization.

A suitable design is an early 100-step block versus a late 100-step block, with exact start steps chosen and frozen after development calibration.

### Crucial recovery-time control

Do not compare one model immediately after its intervention with another model hundreds of steps after release.

For each intervention, measure the organization at the same number of training updates after the block ends.

Use multiple recovery checkpoints.

Also evaluate the models at the same conventional final training step.

Where possible, provide matched post-release learning-rate exposure, since an early intervention may leave more optimization opportunity than a late intervention.

If exact matching is impossible, document what remains confounded.

### Required measurements

- Immediate organization change.
- Organization after equal recovery updates.
- Organization at final training.
- Degree of recovery or reversal.
- Training loss and decision accuracy.
- Whether the original information route remains usable.
- Whether any changed organization is stable.

### Scientific question

**Do early interventions produce more persistent changes than severity-matched late interventions, independently of their immediate magnitude?**

Distinguish:

- Initial change magnitude.
- Persistence.
- Recovery.
- Continued task competence.

Do not call this a critical period unless the data justify that terminology.

# 6. PRIMARY CROSS-FAMILY QUESTION

**Which mathematical and computational principles governing relational information and decisions survive when the relationships between information sources are changed fundamentally?**

This question has several competing possibilities.

### H1 — Common abstract principle, different implementation

T4 and GATE may instantiate a shared high-level principle, even if their required relational variables and neural circuits differ.

### H2 — Shared information-access distinction

Both environments may exhibit information that is present in the neural state but inaccessible to the downstream decision mechanism under a particular restriction.

### H3 — Different relational algorithms

The two environments may require fundamentally different mathematical computations, and attempts to force a shared abstraction may fail.

### H4 — T4-specific phenomenon

Some findings from T4 may be consequences of its source generator, supervision, representation format, or architecture rather than broader relational properties.

### H5 — Shared methodological constraints

Even when the neural computations differ, the same causal-identification limitations may arise: subspace ambiguity, redundant pathways, support restrictions, or non-diagnostic interventions.

These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive.

Design experiments capable of distinguishing them.

# 7. CROSS-FAMILY MATHEMATICAL INVESTIGATION

**Mandatory.**

Before comparing neural activations, derive the relevant mathematical structure of both environments.

## 7.1 Define the relational objects

For each environment identify:

- Information sources.
- Observed primitive inputs.
- Latent relationships, where present.
- Candidate decisions.
- Correct decision rule.
- Minimal or restricted sufficient statistics.
- Conditions under which relevant information is lost.
- Conditions under which a previously sufficient representation becomes insufficient.

Use the actual generator and target definitions.

Do not assume T4's eligibility indicator E, copying statistic m, or readout organization exists in GATE.

## 7.2 Seek a common mathematical statement

Investigate whether both environments support a restricted principle such as:

**Correct relational decisions depend on information about relationships among sources that may not be recoverable from source-wise summaries alone.**

This is a hypothesis, not a theorem.

Determine exactly what it means in each environment.

Construct proofs, counterexamples, or explicit sufficiency conditions.

A meaningful common principle must make nontrivial predictions in both settings.

It is not enough to observe that both tasks involve relationships.

## 7.3 Investigate limits

Seek cases where:

- Source-wise summaries suffice.
- Joint relations are necessary.
- Different relational descriptions are observationally equivalent.
- A representation carries relevant information but an allowed downstream computation cannot use it.
- Changed relational structures require changed decisions.
- A seemingly general principle fails.

Document the assumptions precisely.

A strong counterexample is a legitimate positive research outcome.

Do not inflate a task-specific theorem into a universal claim about all AI systems.

# 17. FINAL SCIENTIFIC DIRECTIVE

The XIII investigations have taught us something essential about scientific methodology.

**A neural experiment can produce an impressive numerical effect without identifying the mechanism responsible for it.**

Several earlier COR interpretations had to be corrected because the measurements did not distinguish the favored explanation from an alternative.

That process was scientifically valuable.

But COR must not remain indefinitely confined to correcting mechanisms in one small generator.

The next major uncertainty concerns **generality**.

We need to know whether the surviving mathematical and causal principles extend to genuinely different relational decision problems.

Therefore:

**First**, complete the missing T4 controls without opening another unlimited local investigation.

**Second**, preserve an accurate scientific record of what the T4 experiments established, falsified, and left unresolved.

**Third**, investigate GATE as a distinct relational environment, using mathematically appropriate definitions and the corrected causal toolkit.

**Fourth**, make at least one prospective cross-family prediction and test it against a genuine competing explanation.

**Fifth**, preserve failures and counterexamples. Do not force a common theory when the environments disagree.

A finding that two environments require different relational information or different computational mechanisms is as valuable as a finding that they share a common principle, provided the difference is rigorously established.

The research objective is not to accumulate confirmations.

It is to determine **which mathematical and computational principles govern the use of relationships among information sources, and under what conditions those principles hold or fail**.

This is the next multidimensional direction for COR.

Remain in **Phase 1.5, Step 1**, with the provisional foundational-exploration estimate unchanged at approximately **25%**.

Do not select a final paper thesis, close the stage, or claim validation of the original obstruction/discrepancy framework.

**Execute Investigation XIV, complete the bounded local controls, investigate the new relational environment, obtain adversarial review, and deliver the verified research archive.**
