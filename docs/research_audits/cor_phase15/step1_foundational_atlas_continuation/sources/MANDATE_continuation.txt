# 2. FIRST CORRECTION: PP1 IS NOT YET AN IMPLEMENTATION-INDEPENDENT CAUSAL LAW

The previous atlas reported PP1 passing in all ten T4-capable models.

That result remains a valid prospective success under its frozen evaluation protocol.

However, its theoretical interpretation is substantially narrower than some of the atlas language suggests.

## 2.1 The evaluation population was selected

In the original PP1 run:

- 135,000 source edits were attempted.
- 41,430 made the exact T4 Bayesian posterior undefined.
- Those edits were excluded.
- A further Bayesian margin-change filter left 47,005 scored edits.

The reported sign agreement is therefore a statement about a **selected set of supported, sufficiently consequential edits**.

It is not a theorem for arbitrary interventions.

Furthermore, an edit that produces an observation with positive *marginal* probability need not be reachable conditional on the particular latent configuration of the original episode.

You must formally separate:

1. Positive marginal support.
2. Positive support conditional on the latent generating state.
3. Naturally reachable input changes under an explicit structural intervention.
4. Surgical edits to observed variables.
5. Surgical edits to internal computational variables.

These are not interchangeable intervention domains.

## 2.2 Derive the real calibration-to-sign theorem

The previous review suggested that PP1 follows from the models being close to the Bayesian posterior.

That is plausible but is not automatically a theorem.

Small average predictive KL divergence does not guarantee correct signs of margin changes on a rare, intervention-selected subset.

Construct a precise result.

For an intervention pair `(x, x')`, define:

- The exact Bayesian candidate-margin change.
- The model's corresponding margin change.
- Pointwise approximation errors at both endpoints.
- The required margin-separation condition.

Derive sufficient conditions under which their signs must agree.

Investigate whether bounds on probability error, log-probability error, total variation, or KL can establish the required conclusion.

Account for candidate probabilities approaching zero.

Determine whether an average-distribution bound can be converted into a high-probability sign guarantee on the intervention distribution.

If this requires density-ratio, support-overlap, tail, or margin assumptions, state them explicitly.

Then construct counterexamples showing why those assumptions matter.

## 2.3 Test against the actual PP1 data

Use the saved PP1 edit population.

Stratify by:

- Bayesian margin-change magnitude.
- Model approximation error.
- Source-relation type.
- Support status.
- Intervention magnitude.
- Architecture and implementation class.

Determine which theoretical sufficient conditions are actually satisfied.

Include at least one deliberately poor but high-accuracy model that violates the sign prediction.

Also include models matched for overall accuracy but different in calibration on the intervention distribution.

**Required result:** a corrected mathematical statement explaining when PP1 sign agreement follows from approximation quality and when it does not.

Do not present PP1 as evidence of a new COR law unless something remains beyond that explanation.

# 3. SECOND CORRECTION: PP2 EXTRAPOLATION IS REAL, BUT THE MECHANISM IS UNIDENTIFIED

The previous atlas found models with similar on-distribution behavior and substantially different behavior under the no-colluder shift.

The independent audit reproduced the contrast.

For example, among disagreements between the original and shifted Bayesian decision rules, one Transformer favored the shifted rule much more often, while the direct-rereading Transformer and DeepSets tended to favor the original rule.

This is useful evidence of implementation-dependent extrapolation.

It is not yet a causal explanation of why those models extrapolate differently.

## 3.1 Separate support from distribution change

The no-colluder shift has limited overlap with the original exact T4 generator.

In an independent sample, only approximately 9.2% of the shifted episodes lay within the original generator's support.

Therefore distinguish:

- Reweighting within common support.
- Shifts of latent priors within a shared support.
- Changes in conditional source distributions.
- Changes in collusion probability or copying rate.
- Changes that introduce inputs impossible under training.
- Changes in the optimal posterior decision.

Derive how each shift affects the Bayesian reference.

The earlier partial-collusion shifts did not adequately distinguish the competing decision rules.

Do not repeat nondiscriminating comparisons.

Choose shifts by first computing where the rival hypotheses make different predictions.

## 3.2 Investigate the actual internal reason for extrapolation

Candidate explanations include:

- Global inference with a configuration-wide latent-state constraint.
- Local per-group reliability inference.
- Factorized reliability decisions.
- Direct cross-question rereading.
- Information bottlenecks that discard or preserve particular dependencies.
- A learned approximation that is accurate on the training distribution but unconstrained elsewhere.

These hypotheses are not equivalent.

Define independently measurable computational signatures for them.

Do not classify a mechanism merely by counting attention heads or finding a visually plausible attention pattern.

Use causal experiments where feasible.

For example, investigate whether source information from one group can influence another group's inferred reliability in ways required by a global configuration constraint.

Use valid in-support controls before applying extrapolation tests.

## 3.3 Make a genuinely prospective prediction

This is one of the highest-leverage investigations.

Before training or evaluating new held-out models, freeze a structural predictor of extrapolation behavior based only on training-distribution computations.

The predictor may use a causal or functional signature of global versus local information integration.

It must not use outcomes from the held-out distribution.

Test it on new independent seeds and, where appropriate, additional architectures.

Compare against:

- Training accuracy.
- Training KL.
- Calibration.
- Model size.
- Head count.
- Random baselines.
- Simpler behavioral diagnostics.
- A flexible predictor with the same available information.

Avoid predicting extrapolation by reading the extrapolation outcomes themselves.

If the predictor succeeds, determine what information it adds and whether it generalizes to more than the original no-colluder shift.

If it fails, preserve the result and characterize the indistinguishable implementations.

**Required result:** either a validated structural prediction of extrapolation or a clear negative finding delimiting what existing structural information can identify.

This is an important opportunity, but it must not consume the whole research program.

# 4. THIRD CORRECTION: PP3 DID NOT IDENTIFY THE TRANSFERRED FEATURE

The earlier PP3 experiments showed:

- T1 pretraining supports later T4 learning.
- T0 and T5 pretraining do not provide the same benefit.
- Resetting T1's last layer leaves successful transfer possible.
- Resetting its early layers defeats transfer in the tested setup.
- The saved-weight controls indicate that learned early layers plus embeddings can support T4 after training a new last layer.

These are meaningful results.

But they do not identify the content of the transferred computation.

The transferred feature could involve:

1. Parsing source pairs.
2. Representing source identities.
3. Detecting within-question agreement.
4. Building cross-question same-group connections.
5. Computing agreement counts.
6. Preparing an attention-routing scaffold.
7. Some combination of these.

The previous experiments did not separate them.

## 4.1 Design discriminating pretraining tasks

Create several controlled training objectives.

At minimum:

**P-local:** Each question has its own independently resampled latent reliability state. Within-question source-pair parsing is useful, but cross-question reliability sharing is not.

**P-group:** Group identities and cross-question relationships are useful, but an exact reliability posterior is not required.

**P-count:** The model benefits from computing a reusable count or relationship statistic, but not from solving the final T4 candidate decision.

**P-reliability:** Genuine shared-reliability inference is useful.

**P-control:** An unrelated task matched for training duration, source complexity, and overall success.

These are candidate experimental designs, not guaranteed discriminating tasks.

Before training, derive which computational information each objective requires and what shortcuts remain possible.

Use matched architecture and optimization settings.

## 4.2 Determine which feature transfers

Use frozen early layers, partial resets, feature ablations, causal interchange, and readout retraining.

Separate:

- Representational sufficiency.
- Optimization initialization.
- Source parsing.
- Cross-question relational information.
- Candidate-specific routing.

Probe identified features using independent source labels and controlled held-out examples.

Do not infer a feature's identity from the fact that preserving its layer preserves transfer.

## 4.3 Investigate learning dynamics directly

The previous explanation that T4 from scratch fails because the monotone agreement shortcut is harmful was withdrawn after review.

Do not restore that explanation without evidence.

Investigate gradients, feature development, attention routing, and learning trajectories in the actual architectures.

Compare early training from random initialization with T1-pretrained initialization.

If proposing an optimization barrier, define its measurable signature.

A loss-curve plateau alone is insufficient to establish a particular mechanism.

**Required result:** a causal or experimentally discriminated explanation of what T1 pretraining contributes to T4 learning, or a precise account of the ambiguity that remains.

# 12. DEVELOP THE STRONGEST POSSIBLE ABSTRACT CAUSAL CLASS

- Explicit pooled summaries.
- Source-token reliability tags.
- Distributed key/query information.
- Redundant routing heads.
- Direct cross-question rereading.

**Source observations → relational information → decision-relevant evidence transformation → candidate response.**

- Conditions under which a relational statistic is sufficient.
- Conditions under which a specific candidate-margin response follows.
- Whether the result survives alternative implementations.
- Which aspects can be identified without evaluating the target outcome.
- What the theorem predicts beyond the input/output function already known.

Try to prove something genuinely informative.

Do not settle for the identity that a deterministic neural network's output is a function of its input.

## 12.3 Attack the abstraction

Construct counterexamples involving:

- Same relation, different candidate decisions.
- Same output function, different internal mechanisms.
- Equivalent functions with different localized mediators.
- Bypass and redundant paths.
- Different source semantics producing opposite decision effects.

## 12.4 Determine whether the class is distinctive

- Causal abstraction.
- Bayesian sufficient-statistic computation.
- Conditional information routing.
- Ordinary nonlinear feature interactions.
- Gating.
- Source-dependency inference.
- Established path-specific causal analysis.

**Required result:** a theorem-and-counterexample account of the broadest nontrivial causal class the evidence supports, or a clear demonstration that the attempted unification fails.

# 20. WHAT COUNTS AS REAL PROGRESS

Substantial progress includes:

- A valid theorem connecting approximation error to intervention-sign agreement under precise support and margin assumptions.
- An operation-level account of seed 12 or another nonlocalized mechanism.
- A causal abstraction that survives multiple genuinely different implementations and meaningful interventions.
- A demonstrated impossibility of a proposed shared abstraction.
- A verified identification method robust to head splitting and relevant gauge transformations.
- A causal explanation of the transferable early-layer computation.
- A genuinely prospective structural predictor of extrapolation.
- A mathematical characterization of source-relation evidence across multiple generative regimes.
- A learned mechanism under unknown or changing source groups.
- A decision-related relational mechanism in a task fundamentally different from latent reliability.
- A general conditional theorem whose assumptions are independently inspectable.
- A rigorous counterexample eliminating an attractive but false generalization.
- A new method or prediction that established Bayesian and mechanistic alternatives do not already supply.

These results need not all be positive.

A mathematically significant negative result is progress.

A successful model without an understood mechanism is preliminary evidence.

A theorem with outcome-dependent assumptions is not a causal explanation.

A new label for an existing Bayesian formula is not a new theory.
