# COR Phase 1.5 Step 1 — Learned Causal Mediator Identification

Continue from `step1_mechanism_identification_continuation`. Do not restart the research or create additional hand-designed agreement circuits as the principal result.

**Research target:** Identify and causally validate the computation responsible for source-relationship-sensitive decisions in the already trained synthetic Transformers, then determine whether a consistency-based explanation contributes anything beyond established nonlinear computation and causal abstraction.

Your previous study established learned task behavior but failed to identify a specific internal mediator. That is now the principal unresolved research problem.

## A. First, make the learned evidence reproducible

The previous release omitted trained checkpoints and required an undocumented executable dependency on the earlier package.

Recover or regenerate the learned T1/T4 models, including TF1, TF2, and attention-only variants where relevant.

Save actual model parameters, source generator configurations, seeds, optimizer settings, training logs, and validation records.

Provide a standalone release that runs without an unpinned sibling directory.

If retraining changes the original results, record the differences instead of replacing the historical evidence.

Before any new confirmation tests, freeze the mechanism-identification procedures and independent held-out evaluations.

## B. Define a controlled source-relationship problem

Develop source generators where:

- Source identity and candidate support are externally specified.
- Agreement and disagreement can vary independently of total evidence strength where the data support permits.
- Matched pairs have the same designated aggregate statistic but different source relationships.
- Some tasks genuinely depend on the relationship.
- Other tasks depend only on an aggregate such as \(a+b\).
- Additional tasks make agreement irrelevant or misleading.

Use continuous evidence strengths and multiple candidates where feasible.

Do not select the definition of consistency from the final model margins.

Most importantly, include a strong aggregate-only control such as:

\[
z=a+b,\qquad m=h(z)
\]

for a flexible nonlinear \(h\).

Behavioral order reversals do not identify a relational mechanism because a nonlinear function of z alone can produce them.

The analysis must distinguish the exact aggregate-only hypothesis from a relation-sensitive computation that retains and uses information beyond z.

## C. Localize candidate mediators in the learned models

Use the task-defined source variables to investigate intermediate states.

Search for individual units, small subspaces, attention/MLP pathways, and distributed representations that carry the relevant source relationship.

Do not require a particular signed encoding such as \(\operatorname{sign}(q)=\operatorname{sign}(ab)\).

An affine shift, invertible transformation, or distributed code may represent the same relationship.

The identification procedure must specify:

1. The independently defined relation being sought.
2. The frozen internal site or computational subgraph.
3. The information used to select it.
4. Whether the feature is merely decodable or causally used.
5. Whether identification relies on candidate output information.
6. Relevant function-preserving reparameterization ambiguities.

Freeze the proposed mediator and intervention protocol before final evaluation.

If unit-level localization fails, investigate distributed mediation rather than concluding the mechanism is absent.

## D. Perform real causal mediator tests

For each candidate mediator:

- Change source A while holding source B fixed.
- Change B while holding A fixed.
- Change their relationship while matching a predefined aggregate statistic.
- Patch or interchange the proposed mediator between carefully matched reachable states.
- Ablate or replace it while testing whether the relation-sensitive decision effect decreases.
- Restore it where a meaningful sufficiency intervention can be defined.
- Check whether residual bypasses or alternative pathways explain the output.
- Compare against random, matched-dimensional, and equally informative alternative mediators.

Distinguish ordinary input-level interventions from surgical internal interventions. Report off-manifold interventions separately.

A successful patch is not automatically unique mediation.

Do not infer causality from probe accuracy, attention visualization, or behavioral interaction alone.

## E. Derive the strongest decision-related theorem

From the identified computation, attempt a formal result connecting:

\[
\text{independently specified source relationship}
\]

through:

\[
\text{identified internal computation}
\]

to:

\[
\text{candidate-specific causal response}.
\]

State the exact frozen-model class, source semantics, structural assumptions, readout, intervention family, and prediction.

Control competing residual contributions. The previous MLP theorem established the sign of its branch contribution, not necessarily the total candidate margin.

Do not assume a source-specific normalized coordinate depends only on that source when LayerNorm couples the coordinates.

The theorem must account for actual computation, including bypasses and downstream transformations.

If a full decision-sign theorem is unavailable, provide the strongest valid conditional statement and identify the missing condition.

## F. Establish whether the mechanism is distinctive

Compare with equally informed:

- Aggregate-only nonlinear models.
- Matched-capacity MLP explanations.
- Standard source interactions.
- Path-specific causal mediation.
- Causal abstraction.
- Direct downstream sensitivity.

Determine whether the proposed consistency concept provides an independently testable restriction, transfer prediction, or mechanistic identification advantage.

Do not call an ordinary learned nonlinear gate a novel COR theory merely because it responds to agreement.

Conversely, do not dismiss an actual restricted causal mechanism merely because its mathematics uses familiar operators.

## G. Replication and generalization

Run new held-out seeds and source distributions that were not used to select the mediator.

Test relevant architectures, source strengths, candidate counts, and matched aggregate contrasts.

Preserve failures.

Do not tune the mediator-selection rule on the final evaluation outcomes.

If no identified mediator generalizes, characterize exactly whether the failure is caused by nonidentifiability, redundant mechanisms, task-specific implementation, optimization variability, or an inadequate identification method.

## H. Correct the existing mathematical and verification limitations

As supporting work:

- Narrow the A1* result to the MLP contribution, or explicitly control all competing contributions to the total margin.
- Scope T-RF′ to the monotone single-index class that it actually tests.
- Treat T-MS as a representation-specific diagnostic, not a universal necessity.
- Correct any unsupported interval-certification language.
- Re-review the results introduced after the previous adversarial reviews.

These corrections must not consume the bulk of the research effort.

If no, determine what failed and why. Do not replace the missing mediator with a behavioral statistic.
