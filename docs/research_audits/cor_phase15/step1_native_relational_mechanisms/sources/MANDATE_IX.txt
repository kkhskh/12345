## 2.1 What Investigation VIII established

Investigation VIII provides evidence that purpose-built neural readers can learn decision-relevant cross-group relational computations without receiving the exact eligibility factor as an input.

Across six new confirmation runs:

- Three attention readers succeeded.
- Three pairwise relational networks succeeded.
- Training used ordinary candidate-label supervision.
- The readers received exact precomputed leave-question agreement counts and current-question source reports.
- Their mean per-episode absolute R1 response error was approximately 0.176.
- Their overall KL divergence to exact Bayes was approximately 0.00079.

The result demonstrates learning of the **cross-group combination step from already-extracted primitive evidence**.

It does not establish end-to-end learning from the original raw token sequence.

The experiment also showed that a feature can be highly decodable without being correctly used.

Failed 16,000-step MLP readers had approximately 0.99 linear decoding accuracy for the eligibility factor, while successful attention readers had approximately 0.83–0.87.

The MLP could succeed under a longer development training budget, so the 16,000-step result does not prove an architectural impossibility.

Three independent attention-reader interventions demonstrated that cross-group communication pathways causally affected the relational candidate response.

However, the intervention mask used mathematically known eligibility information to identify connections for removal.

Therefore, the result is a restricted causal-pathway finding, not identification of a native Transformer relational circuit.

## 2.2 The critical negative result: bypass rather than native repair

A new relational adapter improved all eight tested P-count lineages.

Per-episode R1 absolute error fell from approximately 0.8165 to 0.1822, and mean KL fell from approximately 0.09397 to 0.00216.

But:

- Removing the adapter reduced accuracy to approximately 33.7%.
- Replacing the frozen original P-count representation with its mean left accuracy near 74% in four tested lineages.
- The adapter received engineered primitive evidence and could construct the decision largely independently of the original model.

The correct interpretation is a successful **parallel relational decision system**, not a verified repair of the original P-count computation.

This distinction is fundamental to Investigation IX.

## 2.3 Additional unresolved results

The prior investigation also showed:

- Partial improvement from last-layer unfreezing, rank-8 LoRA, and additional downstream blocks on a single development parent.
- No established Bayes-level native repair.
- Raw-token from-scratch Transformers that did not learn the relevant response by 6,000 steps.
- An interrupted longer raw-token training run.
- Limited held-out group-pair generalization, with only one seed per reader architecture.
- A pairwise reader that retained the correct R1 sign on the tested held-out configuration.
- Incomplete evidence about whether the original model represents the information necessary for the correct cross-group decision.
- No established connection between these learned relational mechanisms and the original COR obstruction/discrepancy operators.

Do not exaggerate any of these results.

## 2.4 Mathematical correction that must be preserved

Use the correct Bayesian eligibility definition:

`m_g = Σ_(h≠g) 1[n_h = 5] · 1[the two sources in group h agree at target question q]`

There is **no additional requirement** that group h's candidate vote match group g's vote.

Verify this formula against the actual generator and exact Bayesian reference.

Do not reintroduce the previously incorrect vote-matching factor.

Also preserve the distinction between:

- Mathematical source-role eligibility.
- Represented agreement counts.
- A decoded eligibility variable.
- A learned computation using eligibility information.
- The final candidate-specific Bayesian response.

These objects must not be treated as interchangeable without evidence.

# 4. WORKSTREAM A — IDENTIFY THE ORIGINAL P-COUNT FAILURE

This is the immediate mechanistic priority.

The original P-count Transformer fails the R1 response despite having learned useful agreement-count representations.

Determine precisely why.

## A1. Separate competing explanations

Investigate at least the following hypotheses.

**H1 — Information insufficiency**

The learned representation does not contain enough information to determine the correct Bayesian response.

**H2 — Representation accessibility**

The necessary information exists in the network, but it is not available in a usable form at the relevant decision stage.

**H3 — Missing relational operation**

The model represents the necessary ingredients but does not correctly combine information across groups.

**H4 — Incorrect downstream integration**

The model computes some relational information but fails to incorporate it into candidate-specific evidence weighting.

**H5 — Optimization limitation**

The architecture can implement the required operation, but its learned parameters remain in a suboptimal computational regime.

**H6 — Architectural constraint**

The tested architecture or computational depth creates a bottleneck that limits the relevant operation.

**H7 — Competing shortcut**

The model learns a simpler operation that works on most training-distribution episodes but fails at the R1 boundary.

For each hypothesis:

- Define its observable consequences.
- Specify an experiment that distinguishes it from alternatives.
- State which results would falsify it.
- Avoid diagnosing the failure solely from output behavior.

## A2. Information-location analysis

Inspect the original model's:

- Source-token residual states.
- Candidate/readout representations.
- Layerwise residual streams.
- Attention queries, keys, and values.
- Attention output projections.
- MLP activations.
- Distributed subspaces.
- Intermediate decision information.

Use independent generator labels to test the availability of:

- Within-group agreement information.
- Cross-group perfect-agreement configurations.
- Eligibility-relevant relational information.
- Candidate evidence.
- Posterior-relevant information.

Distinguish decodability from functional accessibility and causal use.

Do not conclude that a feature is absent solely because a linear probe fails.

Do not conclude that a feature is causally important solely because a probe succeeds.

## A3. Sufficiency tests

Investigate whether the frozen P-count representations contain enough information for a downstream computation to approximate Bayes.

Train controlled readouts using **only native Transformer states**.

No engineered source counts, group reports, eligibility factors, or raw-input side channels may enter these readouts at inference.

Compare:

- Linear readouts.
- Small nonlinear readouts.
- Attention-based state readers.
- Pairwise state-interaction readers.
- Larger-capacity matched controls.

Separate the ability to extract information from the native states from successful repair of the original Transformer.

A powerful downstream model that solves the task using frozen native states establishes representational sufficiency under that reader class.

It does not, by itself, establish that the original model naturally uses the corresponding computation.

# 5. WORKSTREAM B — LEARN THE RELATIONAL OPERATION INSIDE THE ORIGINAL TRANSFORMER

This is the principal experimental extension.

The objective is to determine whether the existing Transformer can develop the relational computation without a parallel decision-making system bypassing it.

2. Selective unfreezing of earlier layers.
3. Full-model fine-tuning.
4. Low-rank adaptation at attention Q/K/V/O projections.
5. Low-rank adaptation at MLP components.

7. Additional native Transformer blocks.
8. A native-state-only downstream attention computation.
9. Matched-capacity ordinary downstream readers.
10. From-scratch raw-token Transformers.
11. Curriculum-trained models with separately specified relational learning objectives.

The native-only experimental conditions must not receive externally calculated counts, current-question group-report features, the eligibility factor, or other engineered relational side information at inference.

If explicit supervision of a relational intermediate is explored, make it a separately labeled experimental condition.

A model trained with an auxiliary eligibility target must not be described as discovering eligibility solely from ordinary candidate supervision.

## B2. Prevent bypass

Every successful native-repair claim must survive tests designed to expose alternative information pathways.

Use:

- Parent-state replacement.
- Native-state resampling from appropriate comparison episodes.
- Layer-specific interventions.
- Full downstream recomputation.
- Matched-state negative controls.
- Candidate-evidence-preserving relational edits.
- Relational-information-preserving evidence edits.
- Comparisons against side-channel and bypass baselines.

Determine whether changing the relevant parent representation changes the repaired decision in the predicted direction.

A readout that ignores the parent representation is not a native repair.

A reader that consumes native states but reconstructs the entire answer using an unrelated information pathway may establish state sufficiency without establishing repair of the intended mechanism.

Report these categories separately.

## B3. Separate types of successful learning

Classify outcomes as:

**Type I:** External bypass solves the task.

**Type II:** Frozen native states contain sufficient information for a new downstream computation.

**Type III:** Modified native network layers learn a useful new relational computation.

**Type IV:** A causally identified native pathway computes and integrates the relational operation.

**Type V:** The identified mechanism generalizes under independently specified changes and supports prospective predictions.

Do not treat these categories as equivalent.

Type IV or V would constitute especially valuable progress, but lower-level results remain scientifically useful.

## B4. Use independent models

Replicate the central findings across genuinely independent pretrained parents and training seeds.

Use at least five independent parents or seeds for the principal comparison where resources permit, with a clear account of actual independence.

If resources prevent this, prioritize a smaller, statistically interpretable design and explicitly restrict the conclusions.

Do not count multiple fine-tunes of one parent as independent pretrained models.

Use matched training budgets and capacity controls.

Preserve all seeds and failed runs.

# 8. WORKSTREAM E — MATHEMATICAL THEORY OF RELATIONAL COMPUTATION

This is a major foundational workstream, not merely an explanation of successful experiments.

Develop mathematically precise results concerning the information requirements, computational structure, and limits of relational decision mechanisms.

## E1. Information sufficiency

Determine which statistics are sufficient for the exact Bayesian decision in the relevant source-generating environments.

Investigate whether:

- Individual agreement counts suffice.
- Joint group configurations are required.
- Marginal source statistics lose relevant information.
- Pairwise relations are sufficient.
- Higher-order interactions are necessary.
- Direct access to raw source evidence changes the computational requirements.

Provide explicit assumptions and counterexamples.

Distinguish statistical sufficiency from what a particular architecture can learn or efficiently implement.

## E2. Limits of separable computation

Extend the restricted additive/separable reader results.

Characterize when a computation of the form

`F(x₁, ..., x_G) = ρ(Σ_g φ_g(x_g))`

can or cannot represent the required relational decision.

State restrictions on:

- The representation dimension.
- The permitted functions.
- The group variables.
- The readout class.
- The number of groups.
- The candidate-decision semantics.

Be careful: sufficiently expressive aggregation architectures may represent interaction functions.

Do not claim a general impossibility merely from the failure of a restricted additive or linear class.

Derive meaningful lower bounds or conditional separation results where possible.

## E3. Computational resources

Investigate whether relational decisions impose measurable requirements on:

- Information accessibility.
- Interaction depth.
- Communication across groups.
- Representation capacity.
- Attention structure.
- Nonlinear composition.
- Intermediate state.

Where possible, connect these requirements to controlled learned-model experiments.

Do not mistake optimization failure for an architectural lower bound.

## E4. Causal factorization

Investigate whether successful relational computations admit a useful abstraction of the form:

`Source evidence → relational operation → evidence transformation → candidate decision`

This is a hypothesis, not an established COR law.

Determine when such a factorization is identifiable and when multiple distinct causal decompositions explain the same behavior.

Test alternatives involving:

- Direct classification.
- Distributed representations.
- Redundant computation.
- Direct rereading.
- Context-conditioned evidence weighting.
- Computation without an explicit relational mediator.

Any proposed theorem must have independently specified assumptions and a falsifiable conclusion.

## E5. Representation invariance

Investigate whether relational computations can be compared across models without requiring identical neuron coordinates, head assignments, token locations, or activation representations.

Distinguish:

- Behavioral equivalence.
- Information equivalence.
- Functional interchangeability.
- Causal abstraction equivalence.
- Mechanistic implementation equivalence.

Determine which claims survive invertible reparameterizations, head splitting, redundant pathways, or alternative circuit decompositions.

A shared decoded variable alone does not establish causal equivalence.

## E6. Predictive theorems

Develop restricted theorems that predict candidate-specific responses under independently defined changes to relational evidence.

Specify:

- The model or computation class.
- The generative assumptions.
- The relational statistic.
- The intervention.
- The competing pathways.
- The exact predicted response.
- The domain where the conclusion holds.

Avoid circular assumptions that already encode the desired response sign.

An elementary theorem with broad empirical coverage can be useful, but its mathematical and experimental contributions must be distinguished.

A successful Investigation IX does not require every workstream to produce a positive result.

Meaningful outcomes include:

- Showing that original P-count states contain sufficient information for correct relational decisions.
- Identifying the specific information missing from those states.
- Demonstrating native relational computation after controlled adaptation.
- Causally identifying the operation through which the original Transformer combines source groups.
- Explaining why highly decodable relational information is not always used.
- Identifying a reproducible learning or architectural bottleneck.
- Finding a native mechanism that differs fundamentally from the small reader's implementation.
- Demonstrating a generalizing relation computation on unseen source configurations.
- Establishing mathematical limits on separable evidence processing.
