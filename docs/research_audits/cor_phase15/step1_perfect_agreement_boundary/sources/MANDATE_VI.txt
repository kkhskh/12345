\[

Across the previously evaluated successful models, its fitted value was higher for T1-lineage models than P-count models.

**T1 and P-count pretraining induce a reproducible difference in the observable candidate-decision response to the 4/5→5/5 source-agreement transition, beyond the difference expected from exact Bayes and ordinary model-approximation error.**

# 3. FIRST PRIORITY: REPAIR THE MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION

The independent audit of Investigation V identified an error in Proposition 3 of `FORMAL_DECISION_EQUIVALENCE.md`.

The proposition claimed that zero response to every minimal Bayesian-null count edit requires:

1. An affine reliability score on counts 0–4.
2. A hard collusion gate.

The necessity of a hard gate is false under its stated model class.

An explicit counterexample is:

\[
\phi_{\mathrm{rel}}(n)=0
\quad(n=0,\ldots,4),
\]

\[
\phi_{\mathrm{rel}}(5)=1,
\]

\[
\phi_{\mathrm{col}}(n)=0
\quad(n=0,\ldots,5).
\]

The collusion gate is finite.

For the relevant minimal Bayesian-null edits that alter only subperfect counts, the mixture weights remain unchanged.

Nevertheless, edits involving count 5 can produce nonzero candidate responses.

This violates the claimed necessity of a hard gate.

## Required mathematical work

A. Independently verify this counterexample using the exact model definition.

B. Withdraw or correct the unrestricted necessity claim.

C. Determine the strongest valid characterization of null-response invariance.

D. Explicitly handle degenerate score functions and limits of the parameter family.

E. If a stronger theorem requires strict monotonicity, nondegeneracy, positive sensitivity, or other assumptions, state and prove those conditions.

F. Distinguish properties of the exact T4 Bayesian posterior from properties of a fitted neural surrogate.

Also revisit the separate quantifier issue in Proposition 1 identified in the independent audit: the theorem statement must respect restrictions imposed by the minimum positive probability in the fixed finite-support generator.

These are necessary repairs.

# 4. DERIVE THE EXACT 4/5→5/5 BAYESIAN RESPONSE

This is the mathematical core of the new experiment.

The original T4 environment contains:

- Candidate-bearing source reports.
- Source groups.
- Reliable-source roles.
- Noise.
- Copying or collusion roles.
- Latent configurations governing how agreement should be interpreted.

The transition from four agreements to five is not necessarily an ordinary increment in evidence.

It may change whether a group is interpreted as reliable, colluding, or copying.

## 4.1 Define the response precisely

For the target question q, let:

\[
V=n^{(-q)}
\]

be the leave-question agreement-count vector.

For a selected candidate pair \(c,c'\), define the model response:

\[
\Delta_{45}m_M
=
m_M(c,c';X_q,V_5)
-
m_M(c,c';X_q,V_4).
\]

Define the corresponding exact Bayesian response:

\[
\Delta_{45}m_B.
\]

The primary error quantity should be:

\[
E_{45}(M)
=
\Delta_{45}m_M-\Delta_{45}m_B.
\]

Here \(m_M\) and \(m_B\) must be defined unambiguously.

Use candidate log-probability ratios as the principal margin measure.

Also evaluate probabilities as a separate, prespecified outcome.

Do not treat arbitrary logit scaling as a causal mechanism.

## 4.2 Characterize the Bayesian decision boundary

Derive the exact response for relevant source configurations.

Investigate:

- When reaching perfect agreement increases inferred reliability.
- When it decreases reliability because of collusion or copying.
- When it changes which latent role is favored.
- When it reverses candidate preference.
- When the effect is negligible.
- When the candidate's own evidence changes the sign.
- How the response depends on other groups' agreement counts.

A 4→5 transition should not automatically be assigned a positive sign.

The source-generating semantics determine the correct effect.

Where possible, derive exact inequalities separating these regimes.

Where not possible analytically, use exact enumeration with verified numerical boundaries.

## 4.3 Distinguish the information change from the decision change

The relevant mathematical object is not merely the count increment.

It is the effect of that increment on the candidate-conditioned source-role posterior and then on the candidate decision.

Determine whether there are contexts where:

- Count changes but source-role posterior does not.
- Source-role posterior changes but candidate probabilities do not.
- Candidate probabilities change without changing the selected candidate.
- Candidate ranking reverses.

Use these distinctions to select experiments.

**Required result:** an exact mathematical reference for the observable 4→5 decision response across the source-role regimes relevant to T4.

# 15. WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A SIGNIFICANT RESULT?

There are several legitimate possible outcomes.

## Outcome A — Prospective curriculum-dependent boundary bias

New independent models reproduce the predicted T1 versus P-count difference in observable Bayes-corrected 4→5 responses.

The effect survives appropriate training-stage, quality, support, and output-space controls.

**This would be a genuinely prospective positive finding about curriculum-associated evidence interpretation.**

It would not by itself establish a distinct neural algorithm.

## Outcome B — A causal computational mechanism is identified

In addition to Outcome A, an independently identified computation selectively explains the boundary-response difference.

Targeted interventions change that effect while preserving relevant baseline decisions.

This would be a stronger contribution: a curriculum-associated causal evidence-update computation.

## Outcome C — The fitted parameter ordering does not correspond to a real decision difference

The observed separation vanishes when tested through supported source-level counterfactuals and directly measured candidate responses.

That would establish that the fitted operator was misleading.

## Outcome D — The apparent difference is explained by training stage or approximation quality

The original ordering does not survive matched convergence or posterior-quality comparisons.

That would clarify the learning dynamics and remove a false curriculum-level explanation.

## Outcome E — Different computational implementations realize the same boundary response

The curricula preserve distinct internal representations or pathways but have equivalent decision responses under the relevant intervention family.

This would provide another restricted example of implementation diversity without a behavioral distinction.

## Outcome F — The mechanism or effect remains unidentified

The confirmation cohort is underpowered, the relevant quality ranges do not overlap, or available interventions cannot separate the proposed explanations.

Report exactly what is unresolved.

Do not manufacture a stronger verdict.

# 16. FINAL DIRECTIVE

Our earlier research established that Transformers can learn source relationships, use them in candidate decisions, and implement the same relational information through different pathways.

But we have repeatedly encountered an important limitation:

**Representing a relationship is not the same as interpreting its decision significance.**

The next experiment targets that distinction.

Both T1 and P-count models can represent source-agreement counts.

The question is whether their training objectives make them interpret perfect agreement differently—particularly where perfect agreement may indicate copying or collusion instead of independent reliability.

You must establish the Bayesian mathematics first.

Then freeze a genuinely prospective, observable decision-response prediction.

Train independent models.

Complete matched transfer schedules in both curricula.

Test valid source-level interventions.

Control for Bayesian approximation and output scaling.

Identify the actual causal operation if a response difference survives.

Challenge the result with alternative pooling models, null controls, and mathematical counterexamples.

Do not repeat the failed null-response-correlation argument.

Do not infer new causal principles from mere Bayes-closeness.

Do not claim a new algorithm from a fitted parameter whose value is not identified.

Do not generalize from one agreement boundary to every relational task.

And do not turn this research into a completion assessment.

**The central scientific deliverable is a defensible answer to:**

### Does relational pretraining change not only what a Transformer knows about agreement, but how it uses that agreement to update candidate decisions?

Establish the answer prospectively, mathematically, and causally, as far as the available evidence permits.
