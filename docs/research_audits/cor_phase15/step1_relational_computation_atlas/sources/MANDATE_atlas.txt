# 0. THE SCIENTIFIC PROBLEM REMAINS OPEN

The original COR ambition was to connect internal computational relationships among representations to candidate-specific Transformer decisions.

The old obstruction score \(O/D\) never established that bridge. Its original fatal problems remain governing constraints.

Recent research has made meaningful progress:

- Explicit agreement-sensitive Transformer-like circuits were constructed.
- Nonlinear MLP, Q/K routing, and multi-layer rereading mechanisms were identified in engineered systems.
- Small trained Transformers were investigated.
- Earlier mediator searches were found to confuse answer representations, source representations, and genuine relational computation.
- A latent-reliability environment gave source relationships an independently defined probabilistic role.
- Learned Transformers were found to use reliability-related information in candidate decisions.
- Under a T1→T4 curriculum, models learned to handle misleading agreement caused by collusion.
- Preregistered replication supported multiple causal components of the mechanism.
- Different successful seeds implemented the same task through different internal pathways.

These are established research directions, not an established unified theory.

**Do not turn them into an assumed final thesis.**

In particular, the following hypotheses must remain distinguishable:

H1. The observed result is ordinary Bayesian reliability inference implemented by attention.

H2. It belongs to a broader class of relation-conditioned candidate-decision computations.

H3. Such mechanisms admit a meaningful implementation-independent causal characterization.

H4. A subset of such mechanisms can be identified independently from frozen model computation.

H5. A consistency-oriented mathematical description provides useful predictions beyond standard Bayesian and mechanistic accounts.

H6. The original COR \(O/D\) framework captures some of this structure under additional independently justified assumptions.

H7. The observed mechanism is an isolated task-specific phenomenon with limited generalization.

Do not presume any hypothesis true.

Investigate which survive and where.

# 2. BUILD AN ATLAS, NOT A COLLECTION OF CASE STUDIES

The central research product is a mathematically organized map of the phenomenon.

For every candidate mechanism or computational class, identify:

- The source information available.
- The independently defined relationship among sources.
- The computational operations capable of using that relationship.
- Where relevant information is represented.
- Whether the relationship is represented explicitly or computed implicitly.
- Which candidate-specific decisions it can influence.
- Which causal interventions reveal its use.
- What it cannot explain.
- Which alternatives produce the same behavior.
- Which parameters and assumptions determine the direction of response.
- The conditions under which the effect disappears or reverses.
- How the mechanism is related to others in the atlas.

A simple label such as "agreement circuit" or "reliability head" is insufficient.

Classify mechanisms by actual computational dependency.

A useful preliminary classification is:

**Class A — Explicit relational mediator.** An intermediate variable encodes a relationship and is read downstream.

**Class B — Distributed relational computation.** Relational information is spread across multiple representations or pathways.

**Class C — On-demand relation computation.** A later component rereads source states and computes the needed relationship during the decision.

**Class D — Implicit relational operation.** The relationship influences Q/K compatibility, nonlinear normalization, gating, or another operation without a separately stored representation.

**Class E — Functionally equivalent non-relational description.** An alternative computation produces the same decisions without the proposed explicit consistency abstraction.

These are provisional categories. Revise them if computation-grounded evidence requires it.

A classification is scientifically useful only if it distinguishes something causally, mathematically, or predictively meaningful.

Do not create categories merely to organize a report.

# 3. RESEARCH DIRECTION A — ALTERNATIVE IMPLEMENTATIONS AND COMPUTATIONAL EQUIVALENCE

The most immediate theoretical problem comes from the successful seeds.

Different Transformer mechanisms appear to perform essentially the same source-reliability-dependent decision function.

We need to understand their relationship.

### A1. Reconstruct the implementations

Analyze at least:

1. The localized source-token tag followed by later routing.
2. The split key/query implementation from the earlier seeds.
3. Seed 10's redundant three-head routing.
4. Seed 12's direct cross-question rereading.
5. DeepSets' explicit pooled computation.
6. The group-histogram MLP as a competing realization.
7. Appropriate engineered causal controls from prior work.

For each implementation, reconstruct the actual causal computational graph.

Do not assume that attention weights alone describe the computation.

Include values, output matrices, residual paths, normalization, nonlinear operations, and downstream readout.

### A2. Determine what the implementations have in common

Possible commonalities include:

- Dependence on cross-question relational evidence.
- An intermediate sufficient statistic.
- A reliability-conditioned evidence-weighting operation.
- A change in candidate margins conditional on source reliability.
- A particular information bottleneck.
- A causal factorization shared across different representations.

Investigate these possibilities.

Do not define the commonality so broadly that every arbitrary classifier qualifies.

A statement such as "the model computes a function of the input" is useless.

### A3. Derive an equivalence notion

Distinguish:

- Same input/output function.
- Same behavior on the tested distribution.
- Same abstract intermediate variables.
- Same causal response under selected interventions.
- Same computational factorization.
- Same internal mechanism up to a valid reparameterization.

These are different equivalence relations.

Attempt to construct a mathematically useful equivalence class for the observed mechanisms.

Determine what is preserved under the equivalence and what can differ.

Try to build a formal causal abstraction that includes both an explicit tag-and-route implementation and a direct-rereading implementation.

If no common causal abstraction survives meaningful interventions, construct a counterexample and explain the obstacle.

### A4. Redundancy and competing pathways

Analyze models where several attention heads carry overlapping reliability information.

Determine whether:

- Individual heads are necessary.
- Sets of heads are jointly necessary.
- Their contributions compensate for each other.
- An ablation creates a distribution shift.
- Earlier layers can reconstruct information lost by a patch.
- Different pathways implement the same functional role.

Use seed 10 as an actual test case.

Develop intervention methods that are not restricted to an arbitrary fixed count of top heads.

Report how the conclusion depends on the selected intervention set.

**Target result:** a defensible account of the computational properties shared across heterogeneous successful implementations, or an explicit demonstration that the similarities are insufficient to define a common causal class.

# 4. RESEARCH DIRECTION B — WHAT COUNTS AS A SOURCE RELATIONSHIP?

The word "consistency" currently covers several different mathematical objects.

We must separate them.

Investigate at least these relationship families:

**Statistical agreement:** multiple sources provide matching observations.

**Candidate-relative agreement:** sources independently support the same candidate.

**Reliability evidence:** source relationships change the inferred credibility of their generating processes.

**Constraint compatibility:** several statements or intermediate states jointly satisfy a specified constraint.

**Semantic compatibility:** different evidence representations support mutually compatible interpretations.

**Causal compatibility:** source representations agree with a specified causal model or intervention response.

**Redundancy and dependence:** sources appear to agree because they share a common origin, copied information, or correlated errors.

**Contradiction and conflict:** relationships signal information that must be reconciled rather than aggregated.

For each relation:

1. Define it independently of the evaluated model's candidate logits.
2. Identify which source semantics are required.
3. Determine what information it retains or discards.
4. Derive when it is relevant to a decision.
5. Construct cases where it is irrelevant.
6. Construct cases where its apparent interpretation reverses.
7. Determine whether an actual Transformer computation can respond to it.
8. Determine how the relation differs from a conventional nonlinear feature.

Do not assume all these relations should be combined into one scalar.

Do not assume they require the same computational mechanism.

**Critically:** Latent reliability is one candidate application. Explore whether there are other genuine decision-relevant relational mechanisms that do not reduce to estimating a hidden source's reliability.

Use carefully controlled synthetic settings for those comparisons.

Do not broaden by inventing arbitrary new scores.

Broaden by defining scientifically meaningful relationships and investigating their actual computational consequences.

# 5. RESEARCH DIRECTION C — THE MATHEMATICAL BOUNDARIES OF THE OBSERVED MECHANISM

Develop a serious theory of the latent-reliability mechanism's operating domain.

The present model has fixed source groupings, three candidates, six questions, limited architecture variation, a specific colluder design, and a particular curriculum.

These conditions should not be mistaken for general conditions.

### C1. Bayesian boundaries

Derive the exact reliability posterior and its dependence on:

- Agreement counts.
- Number of sources.
- Number of candidate classes.
- Prior reliability probabilities.
- Source error rates.
- Colluder prevalence.
- Group size.
- Number of questions.
- Correlated errors.
- Alternative source dependency models.

Determine analytically when agreement increases reliability and when it decreases reliability.

For collusion, derive the decision boundary separating regimes where further agreement is evidence for trustworthiness from regimes where it is evidence for copying or coordination.

Test whether the reported T4 non-monotonicity is:

- Specific to the original generator.
- Stable across a parameter region.
- Altered by priors.
- Eliminated by different collusion mechanisms.
- Present under partial rather than perfect collusion.

Do not derive a sweeping theorem from one posterior curve.

### C2. Decision consequences

For candidate c and competitor c′, define:

\[
m_{c,c'}(x)=L_c(x)-L_{c'}(x).
\]

Derive conditions under which changing source relationships changes:

- The inferred reliability.
- The evidence weighting.
- The candidate margin.
- The preference ordering.
- The selected candidate.
- Decision confidence or stability.

Do not conflate changes in reliability with changes in candidate correctness.

Do not assume agreement has a universally positive sign.

The correct response may be positive, negative, zero, or non-monotonic.

The result should be a collection of conditional theorems and counterexamples with clearly defined domains.

### C3. Necessary versus sufficient conditions

For every proposed theorem, determine whether the assumptions are:

- Necessary.
- Sufficient.
- Both.
- Merely convenient for proof.
- Directly inspectable in the frozen computation.
- Equivalent to restating the target outcome.

Investigate competing residual and attention paths.

Prove appropriate bypass and sufficient-bottleneck results.

Construct same-relation/different-decision counterexamples.

Investigate whether a relation statistic plus independently specified candidate evidence is sufficient to predict a margin or decision response.

Do not promote a branch-level sign result into a total-logit sign result without controlling competing pathways.

**Target result:** meaningful mathematical boundaries, not another single favorable sign theorem.

# 8. RESEARCH DIRECTION F — REPRESENTATION INVARIANCE AND MECHANISM IDENTIFICATION

### F1. Function-preserving transformations

Investigate valid transformations involving:

- Query/key changes of basis.

- Redundant units and pathways.
- Unit permutations.
- Residual-stream basis changes with correct treatment of normalization.
- Alternative encodings of reliability information.
- Distributed key/query implementations.

Identify which proposed explanatory quantities remain invariant.

### F2. Distributed representations

Do not make the scientific theory depend on a single reliability neuron or a selected one-dimensional tag.

Develop methods that can identify:

- Subspace representations.
- Redundant representations.
- Distributed feature codes.

- Operation-level relational computations.

- Decodability.

# 12. RESEARCH DIRECTION J — FORMAL CAUSAL-DECISION THEORY

This is one of the most important workstreams.

Try to develop a theorem family that connects independently defined source relationships to candidate decisions through identified computational structure.

A candidate abstract setting may involve:

- Evidence variables \(X_1,\dots,X_n\).
- A defined relationship \(C(X_1,\dots,X_n)\).
- Possibly a latent generating state \(R\).
- A frozen Transformer computation \(F_\theta\).
- An internal relational computation or causal pathway \(G_\theta\).
- Candidate margins \(m_{c,c'}\).
- An intervention family \(\mathcal I\).

These symbols are placeholders, not an assumed theory.

Find the weakest meaningful assumptions that support a genuine decision-related conclusion.

### J1. Representation and sufficiency

Under which conditions does a relational summary preserve all information necessary for a class of decisions?

What additional information is required to distinguish individual candidates?

Can two inputs have the same relational summary but opposite candidate preferences?

If yes, characterize the missing information.

### J2. Causal computation

Under which conditions does an identified relational operation mediate a candidate-margin response?

What changes when the representation is distributed?

What changes when there are bypass paths?

What changes when the same function is implemented by direct rereading?

### J3. Direction and failure boundaries

When does increasing agreement increase candidate support?

When does it decrease support?

When is there no predictable effect?

Which assumptions determine the sign?

Can those assumptions be verified independently of the evaluated decision change?

### J4. Implementation-independent predictions

Can a theorem predict a decision response across several different computational implementations?

If yes, determine what structural properties must be common.

If no, build a counterexample establishing why the proposed abstraction is insufficient.

### J5. Impossibility results

Seek meaningful restricted impossibility results.

Examples could concern:

- Inference from insufficient statistics.
- Nonidentifiability of latent groups.
- Absence of distinct relation information after a bottleneck.
- Inability to uniquely localize redundant causal pathways from observational evidence.
- Universal sign predictions under unconstrained readout weights.
- Function-level attribution without a representation convention.

Do not call a narrow counterexample a universal impossibility theorem.

**Target result:** a coherent collection of positive and negative theorems characterizing the class and its boundaries.

# 13. REEXAMINE ORIGINAL COR WITHOUT PRESUMING IT CAN BE RESCUED

The original framework involved candidate-specific germs, transport/compatibility maps, discrepancies, and the obstruction quantities \(O_c\) and \(D_c\).

The known limitations remain:

- Candidate rankings need not correspond to discrepancy rankings.
- Decreasing discrepancy need not improve the candidate margin.
- The quantities depend on representations, maps, and metrics.
- Exact computational equations can hold for wrong decisions.
- Approximate surrogate residuals can measure modeling error rather than a decision conflict.
- The original discrepancy construction does not automatically establish a nonzero cohomology class.
- Directional claims require noncircular computational assumptions.

Investigate whether any genuinely learned relational mechanism gives the old framework a mathematically justified interpretation.

Do not merely rename Bayesian disagreement as a coboundary.

Do not choose a projection or map because it correlates with the output.

Possible results include:

- A rigorous restricted relationship between an independently defined discrepancy and a learned mechanism.
- A proof that a particular O/D construction loses necessary candidate-directed information.
- An identification theorem requiring additional source semantics.
- A counterexample showing that the same obstruction value corresponds to different reliability-conditioned decisions.
- A determination that the older framework is not useful for the discovered mechanism.

All are legitimate scientific results.

The original ten failure issues—F1–F7, F25, F29, F30—must remain visible.

Do not quietly replace COR with a different theory and present that as having solved the original problem.

Equally, do not force promising new mechanisms into the old formalism if the mathematics does not support that move.

# 17. ADVERSARIAL SCIENTIFIC REVIEW

Conduct serious adversarial review of the substantive results.

Use genuinely separate subagents where the environment supports them.

The review should include at least these distinct roles:

**Formal mathematics:** attacks quantifiers, theorem assumptions, invariance, sufficiency, and impossibility claims.

**Causal identification:** attacks intervention validity, mediation claims, bypasses, matched-state comparisons, and off-manifold artifacts.

**Transformer computation:** verifies the actual Q/K/V, residual, MLP, normalization, and readout pathways.

**Representation theory:** attacks gauge dependence, distributed coding, and unique localization claims.

**Statistical inference:** checks Bayesian derivations, latent identifiability, uncertainty, and distribution-shift conclusions.

**Learning dynamics:** attacks emergence, curriculum, initialization, and sample-complexity explanations.

**Novelty and baselines:** challenges every claim against established theory and stronger alternative explanations.

**Counterexample construction:** deliberately searches outside reported parameter grids and favorable examples.

**Positive-theory defense:** checks whether valid restricted causal theorems are being dismissed simply because they are conditional or use known mathematical ingredients.

A negative review finding should be investigated, not merely acknowledged.

If a new major result is added after review, subject that result to fresh review.

Do not assert independent review where agents share all reasoning or where the reviewer has not examined the final implementation.

The largest danger is producing ten workstreams that never speak to each other.

Every major finding must be integrated into the common scientific picture.

Examples:

- If an unknown-source-group model succeeds, determine which computational mechanisms implement group identification and how that changes the original reliability-routing story.
- If two architectures use different intermediates, determine whether they share a meaningful causal abstraction.
- If a representation changes under a function-preserving transformation, determine whether the mechanism can be described invariantly at a higher level.
- If a collusion regime reverses the relationship between agreement and reliability, determine which theorem assumptions changed.
- If a trained model uses direct rereading rather than an explicit mediator, determine whether the same mathematical decision prediction still applies.
- If a flexible ordinary nonlinear baseline explains an effect, determine exactly what is left of the consistency explanation.
- If a relation statistic proves insufficient, identify what additional information is needed and whether it is computationally accessible.

The integrated result should be a structured network of propositions, counterexamples, mechanism families, and dependencies.

Do not just stack findings chronologically.

# 20. SCIENTIFIC ACCEPTANCE STANDARD

Do not score success by:

- Number of documents written.
- Number of models trained.
- Number of passing unit tests.
- Number of reviewers.
- Number of successful circuits.
- Number of positive mediation results.

Those are supporting measures.

The research is substantively successful if it produces several of the following:

1. A meaningful computational class spanning more than one implementation.
2. A theorem establishing a candidate-decision consequence across that class.
3. A proof of a relevant implementation or identification impossibility.
4. A well-characterized boundary where source relationships help, hurt, or become irrelevant.
5. A causal identification method that works for distributed as well as localized representations.
6. A verified account of alternative learned implementations.
7. A mathematical explanation of when a relationship statistic is sufficient and when it loses critical information.
8. A mechanistic account of why different training regimes produce different computational solutions.
9. A prospective prediction that survives on a genuinely unexamined setting.
10. A strong counterexample that eliminates a tempting but false generalization.
11. An actual distinction between a consistency-based explanation and existing Bayesian/nonlinear accounts.
12. A clearer mathematical statement of which parts of the original COR ambition are possible, conditional, unsupported, or unidentifiable.

These are goals, not assumptions.

If a particular direction turns out to be unproductive, demonstrate why and redirect effort based on the evidence.

Do not falsely report complete exploration of a domain that was barely examined.

# 21. FINAL SYNTHESIS: NO PREMATURE PAPER THESIS

At the end, answer the following.

**Computational structure**

- What distinct classes of relation-sensitive decision computations have been identified?
- Which observed successful circuits belong to the same class?
- Which do not?
- What differentiates their causal pathways?
- Is there a useful invariant across different implementations?

**Mathematical characterization**

- What can be proved about their decision consequences?
- Which assumptions determine the direction and magnitude of response?
- Which assumptions are necessary?
- What are the strongest counterexamples?
- What is mathematically impossible under specified restrictions?

**Generality**

- Under which source-generating processes does the phenomenon arise?
- What changes under collusion, copying, correlated errors, and unknown groups?
- How do candidate count, source count, and context length affect it?
- When does the model compute an explicit intermediate versus a distributed or implicit relation?

**Causality and identification**

- Which internal relations are causally used?
- Which are merely decodable?
- Which conclusions survive valid reparameterizations?
- What can be identified independently of candidate outcomes?
- What cannot be identified uniquely?

**Learning**

- Why does curriculum matter?
- Why do some seeds construct an earlier tag while others reread the evidence?
- Which aspects depend on optimization versus architectural capability?
- What mechanism-selection predictions can be tested prospectively?

**Scientific distinctiveness**

- Which results follow directly from Bayesian inference?
- Which follow from standard nonlinear computation?
- Which involve established causal abstraction or interpretability tools?
- What new theorem, prediction, or method—if any—remains?
- What additional evidence would be necessary to establish something distinctive?

**Original COR**

- Does any independently identified computational relationship connect to the original discrepancy formalism?
- If not, which precise mathematical obstacles remain?
- Which original fatal problems have actually been addressed?
- What broader foundational questions remain open?

Do not force these answers into a single thesis statement.

The output should be an evidence-backed mathematical research map that makes future theoretical progress possible.

# 22. FINAL EXECUTION DIRECTIVE

We have learned that a trained Transformer can use cross-source relationships to influence candidate decisions through reliability-sensitive computation.

We have also learned that successful models need not implement the same localized circuit.

That is the beginning of a broader investigation.

**Do not reduce this research to T4.**

**Do not reduce consistency to agreement counts.**

**Do not reduce causality to activation patching.**

**Do not reduce identifiability to finding a probe direction.**

**Do not reduce Transformer computation to attention-weight correlations.**

**Do not reduce the mathematics to one Bayesian generative model.**

**Do not call a known mathematical mechanism novel merely because it appears in a Transformer.**

**Do not abandon legitimate restricted mechanisms simply because they are not universal.**

**Do not promote the current evidence into a paper thesis.**

Investigate the computational landscape: alternative implementations, structural equivalence, representation invariance, unknown source organization, different reliability regimes, correlated and adversarial evidence, decision boundaries, distributed mediation, optimization dependence, and mathematical generalization.

Find positive results. Find negative results. Find impossibility boundaries. Build counterexamples. Test strong baselines. Develop theorems. Compare implementations. Make predictions that could fail.

Most importantly, connect the results into a coherent mathematical structure rather than an expanding collection of isolated experiments.

We are still within the foundational research stage.

**Your task is to expand and rigorously characterize the scientific territory—not to declare that the territory has already been understood.**

Execute this research as deeply as your available tools and resources permit. Preserve the complete evidence and clearly identify any parts of the map that remain unexamined.
