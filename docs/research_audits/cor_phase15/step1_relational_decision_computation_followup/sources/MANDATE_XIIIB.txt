# 2. WHAT INVESTIGATION XIII ESTABLISHED — AND WHAT IT DID NOT

Investigation XIII produced several important results.

### Established within the tested generator and models

1. The exact Bayesian posterior for the shared-copying generator was derived and verified against brute-force latent-variable enumeration.

2. For one colluder, a compact per-group summary is sufficient across the investigated copying probabilities.

3. For two shared-copying colluders, joint between-group relational information can become necessary. At exact copying, this distinction matters on a restricted subset; at partial copying, it becomes more widespread.

4. Independently trained Transformers exhibited heterogeneous decision organizations.

5. Two fresh complete models, uP161 and uP165, showed a strong final-layer attention-mixture mechanism.

6. Two other complete models, uP160 and uP163, were classified as pre-assembled: much of the decision appeared to be formed before the final layer.

7. uP164 lay near the mixture-class threshold and missed it by approximately 0.001.

8. Attention-row transplants demonstrated partial causal use of mixture weights in the strong-mixture models.

9. Attention-only and MLP-only adaptation of the final block recovered near-Bayesian two-colluder decisions across all five complete parents.

10. Matched-budget DAS training did not reliably distinguish the correct eligibility variable E from the perfectness-only alternative A1.

### Important falsifications and weaknesses

- P5 was falsified: predicted readout-only adaptation behavior did not generalize across solution classes.
- P8 was falsified: E-trained DAS subspaces failed to acquire the expected E-specificity.
- P1 and P2 passed on only two complete parents and would be falsified if borderline uP164 were included.
- P3 was qualified rather than fully discriminating.
- P7 was correlational and did not identify a causal learning mechanism.
- The main attention-mixture pathway mediated only about 25–33% of the total measured effect.
- Attention similarity to Bayesian responsibilities was mainly observational.
- The pre-assembled solution class was not causally reconstructed.
- The joint cross-group statistic required for the two-colluder problem was not causally identified inside repaired networks.
- No substantial same-parent fine-tuning-seed replication was performed.
- Several affordable experiments recommended by the third reviewer were not run.

These limitations are not merely paperwork.

They determine whether the emerging interpretation of COR's relational computations is scientifically defensible.

**The objective of XIII-B is to investigate these unresolved mechanisms, repair weak experimental identification, and produce stronger mathematical and causal conclusions.**

Do not rerun XIII's successful tests without explaining what new hypothesis they distinguish.

# 3. CENTRAL RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Investigate four connected questions.

**Question A — Computational heterogeneity**

Do attention-mixture and pre-assembled Transformers implement the same candidate-evidence computation at different neural locations, or do they learn different algorithms?

**Question B — Natural causal mediation**

Which pathways genuinely mediate relational evidence updates, and which apparent mediators are products of learned subspace alignment, synthetic activation interventions, or weak controls?

**Question C — Structural adaptation**

When a model learns the correct two-colluder rule, does it construct and use the required joint cross-group relation, or does it exploit some different sufficient computation?

**Question D — Learning and replication**

Are the observed solution classes stable across fine-tuning seeds and pretrained lineages, or are they artifacts of thresholds, small sample sizes, or optimization variability?

These questions should guide the experiments.

They are not independent research programs. They are different ways of examining the relationship between relational information, internal computation, and candidate decisions.

# 4. WORKSTREAM A — CAUSALLY RECONSTRUCT THE PRE-ASSEMBLED SOLUTION CLASS

**Highest priority. Mandatory.**

Investigation XIII devoted substantial attention to the mixture models but did not explain how the pre-assembled models make their decisions.

That asymmetry must be corrected.

Use uP160 and uP163 as the main development systems, while retaining uP161, uP165, and uP164 for comparison.

## A1. Locate the candidate-decision calculation

Identify where the candidate-specific decision becomes available and where it becomes causally operative.

Examine:

- Layer-0 and layer-1 attention.
- Layer-1 MLP operations.
- Intermediate residual streams.
- Source-token and readout-token contributions.
- Evidence aggregation.
- Candidate-likelihood transformations.
- Final-layer attention and MLP effects.

Do not infer that a computation is complete merely because its result is linearly decodable before layer 2.

Test whether the relevant state actually causes the downstream candidate decision.

## A2. Identify the algorithm

Compare the pre-assembled model against explicit alternatives:

- Per-group evidence weighting.
- Pooled reliability weighting.
- Pairwise relational combination.
- Candidate-specific lookup or shortcut.
- Distributed mixture computation.
- A computation different from the attention-mixture model.

Use mathematically valid input edits that produce different predictions under these alternatives.

The goal is not simply to discover which layer contains an answer-like feature.

The goal is to identify **what operation is performed**.

## A3. Causal reconstruction

Use controlled path interventions, source edits, component removal, and restoration.

Attempt to establish:

**Source relationships → relational computation → candidate-evidence combination → decision.**

Distinguish computation, transmission, mediation, and downstream readout.

Examine whether the apparent pre-assembled decision can be transferred or restored through its actual causal pathway.

## A4. Compare directly against mixture models

After reconstructing the pre-assembled implementation, compare the two solution classes at an abstract computational level.

Determine whether:

- They use the same sufficient relational information.
- They produce equivalent intervention-response laws.
- They implement equivalent candidate-evidence transformations.
- They differ only in computational location.
- They implement distinct approximation strategies.

Do not describe them as the same algorithm based solely on matching outputs.

Do not describe them as different algorithms merely because their attention patterns differ.

**A complete answer to this workstream is more valuable than another positive attention-mixture correlation.**

# 5. WORKSTREAM B — COMPLETE THE MISSING CAUSAL CONTROLS

- Attention mass.
- Head dimension.
- Baseline output contribution.
- Activation magnitude.
- Intervention magnitude.
- Relevant token positions.

## B2. Balanced E-versus-A1 identification

The previous DAS training distribution rarely separated E from A1.

The E-, A1-, and PERM-trained subspaces largely overlapped.

Repeat the identification experiment using a mathematically valid, balanced collection of relational configurations in which the competing variables make different predictions.

Match:

- Training sample counts.
- Architecture and subspace dimensions.
- Optimization steps.
- Training objectives.
- Evaluation distributions.
- Control intervention strengths.

Include source-independent patches and appropriate null representations.

Ask whether a variable-specific causal abstraction can actually be learned.

A failed result should distinguish insufficient identification from failure of the target variable to mediate the natural computation.

Do not equate successful supervised subspace fitting with unique mediator identification.

## B3. Restoration through an independent pathway

Investigation XIII did not perform a frozen restoration experiment through a pathway distinct from the attention-row transplantation.

Design and execute one.

The experiment must have a discriminating prediction about E versus A1 or another well-defined competing relational computation.

Use a pathway whose intervention effect is not guaranteed by the training objective of the learned subspace.

Require:

- A meaningful causal removal.
- A separate restoration route.
- Candidate-specific effect recovery.
- Matched decoy restoration.
- Preservation of unrelated decision effects.
- No-op and source-free controls.

A restoration that is equally successful with an arbitrary trained decoy is not evidence for the proposed relational mediator.

## B4. Partial and resampling ablations

Investigation XIII used a broad layer-1 mean ablation but did not measure important downstream consequences.

Run graded and resampling interventions.

Measure:

- Residual candidate-margin effects.
- Attention mass.
- Attention entropy.
- Mixture-weight changes.
- Candidate-response calibration.
- The effect of removing individual pathways.
- The effect of removing combinations of pathways.

Determine whether the remaining behavior reflects redundancy, alternative computation, or the side effects of a large ablation.

The behavioural residual must be computed, not merely promised in documentation.

## B5. Decompose the unexplained decision effect

The principal attention-row pathway explained only about 25–33% of the measured effect.

Investigate the remainder through:

- Attention values.
- Other attention heads.
- MLP pathways.
- Residual-state contributions.

- Interactions between these components.

Do not assume the individual contributions add linearly.

Measure interactions and redundancy where appropriate.

A causal explanation of a partial pathway should not be presented as an explanation of the entire decision.

# 6. WORKSTREAM C — DISSECT THE TWO-COLLUDER REPAIR

**Mandatory mathematical and mechanistic investigation.**

Investigation XIII demonstrated that frozen early-layer representations can support near-optimal two-colluder decisions after adapting attention or MLP components in layer 2.

However, it did not identify the computation learned during that adaptation.

This is one of the most valuable unresolved COR questions.

## C1. Identify the required joint relation

Use the exact shared-copying posterior derived in Investigation XIII.

Determine the necessary between-group statistic \(J_H\), or an explicitly equivalent sufficient representation.

Construct valid pairs of episodes in which per-group summaries match but the joint relation differs.

Verify that the exact Bayesian candidate decision changes.

Do not import the earlier incorrect independent-copying posterior.

## C2. Locate the neural information

Investigate whether information about \(J_H\):

- Is present in frozen layers 0–1.
- Is decodable from the layer-2 input.
- Is assembled inside layer 2.
- Appears only after adaptation.
- Is distributed across attention and MLP pathways.
- Is used causally by the repaired decision.

A decodable joint statistic is not automatically a causally used statistic.

## C3. Identify what adaptation changes

Compare existing:

- Attention-only repairs.
- MLP-only repairs.
- Full-block repairs.
- Fresh-block repairs.

Dissect the successful adapted models.

Test whether they reuse the one-colluder computation, construct a new cross-group relation, or compute an alternative sufficient decision rule.

Use valid counterfactual interventions and matched component controls.

## C4. Test necessity, not only sufficiency

Investigation XIII showed that adapting layer 2 is sufficient.

It did not test whether adapting earlier layers while keeping layer 2 frozen could also solve the changed task.

Run a controlled layers-0–1-only adaptation arm with the final layer frozen.

Compare against the existing layer-2 repair results under matched budgets.

If earlier-layer adaptation succeeds, determine how it changes the information presented to the frozen final layer.

If it fails, do not immediately infer mathematical necessity; distinguish optimization limitations from architectural or informational limitations.

## C5. Connect the mechanism to the sufficiency theorem

The central mathematical question is:

**How does a change in source-dependence structure change the relational computation required for a correct candidate decision?**

Determine whether the observed neural repairs follow the predicted sufficiency boundary.

Derive restricted mathematical results or explicit counterexamples where supported.

Do not claim that any one intermediate statistic is universally necessary when an alternative sufficient representation may exist.
