## Central question

**What determines the order in which neural networks learn to use relationships between information sources?**

In particular:

Does a network learn simpler relationships first, does it prioritize relationships that provide larger decision improvements, or does learning order depend on additional properties of optimization and representation?

Investigation XIV created a meaningful opening.

Two newly trained GATE models initially approached the solution available from individual information sources. They then acquired the full gating relation relatively abruptly.

They did not learn the intermediate pairwise-agreement strategy to the degree predicted.

That observation suggests a possible learning transition, but it does not establish a general law.

**The purpose of XV is to determine whether that learning pattern can be predicted when we deliberately change the mathematics of the task.**

Do not merely reproduce the original GATE experiment.

Do not construct a theory after observing new training trajectories and present it as a prospective prediction.

Do not equate a mathematically possible learning stage with a stage neural optimization must actually pass through.

# 2. EXACT STARTING POINT FROM INVESTIGATION XIV

The original GATE generator has a deterministic trust relation identified using two non-answer sources.

The complete correct decision has interaction order three.

XIV established exact reference points, including approximately:

| GATE decision strategy | Expected negative log-likelihood |
|---|---:|
| Best order-1 rule | 0.462 |
| Best order-2 rule | 0.431 |
| Bayes-optimal full rule | 0.182 |

These values apply to the original GATE generator only.

They must not be copied to newly constructed variants.

The improvement from order 1 to order 2 was relatively small, whereas the improvement from the order-2 strategy to the full gating strategy was much larger.

Two new models trained from scratch initially learned source-based evidence without substantial gate use.

The intermediate pairwise-agreement strategy was learned weakly or partially.

The complete gate relation then emerged rapidly.

XIV's mathematical prediction of an initial source-based phase was confirmed.

**The interpretation that greater decision benefit causes the model to skip the intermediate stage is a hypothesis, not an established result.**

This distinction is central to XV.

# 3. DEFINE THE RESEARCH VARIABLES BEFORE EXPERIMENTATION

Three quantities must be distinguished.

## 3.1 Structural relational complexity

Operationally define complexity using the number of sources participating in the relevant relationship.

For each task, calculate:

1. The minimum number of sources needed to determine the trust relation.
2. The minimum interaction order required for the Bayes-optimal candidate decision.
3. The best achievable decision performance for each restricted interaction order.

These are related but not identical.

For example, the original GATE gate depends on two non-answer sources, but its complete decision requires three-way interactions because the gate determines which answer-bearing evidence matters.

Do not confuse **gate arity** with **decision interaction order**.

Define interaction order using a precise mathematical function class, such as the order-limited logit expansion established in XIV.

Verify minimality, not merely that a high-order representation can express the rule.

Where possible, use proofs and exact enumeration.

## 3.2 Decision usefulness

Define usefulness independently of training outcomes.

The principal measure should be the exact expected reduction in optimal negative log-likelihood when a specified relational component becomes available.

For example:

\[
\Delta_k
=
\mathcal L^*_{\mathrm{restricted}}
-
\mathcal L^*_{\mathrm{relational}}
\]

where both quantities are exact or numerically verified population optima under declared function classes.

Also report:

- Bayes accuracy improvement.
- Candidate-decision changes.
- Probability of examples where the relationship matters.
- The improvement attributable to intermediate relationships.
- The improvement attributable to the complete trust relation.

Usefulness must refer to a property of the task, not how much an already trained model improved.

## 3.3 Learning difficulty and learning signal

**Structural complexity and optimization difficulty are not synonymous.**

A more useful relationship may generate a stronger gradient signal and therefore be easier to acquire.

Consequently, changing usefulness may also change effective training difficulty.

XV must explicitly acknowledge and investigate this coupling.

Measure relevant learning-signal properties, including:

- Initial gradient magnitude.
- Gradient alignment with relationally informative parameters.
- Relevant signal-to-noise ratios.
- Early training-loss improvement.
- Sample dependence.
- Optimization stability.

These are mechanistic diagnostics, not automatic substitutes for the mathematical definitions.

Do not claim complexity and usefulness have been fully disentangled merely because they were assigned separate columns in an experiment table.

**The central comparison is structural complexity versus measurable decision benefit, with optimization consequences explicitly measured.**

# 4. MANDATORY COMPETING LEARNING HYPOTHESES

Develop precise versions of the following explanations.

## H1 — Complexity-first learning

Models preferentially acquire lower-order relational computations before higher-order ones.

Under otherwise comparable conditions, increasing interaction order should delay the acquisition of the relevant relation.

An intermediate lower-order strategy should often emerge before a higher-order strategy, provided it is useful and learnable.

## H2 — Decision-benefit learning

Models preferentially acquire relationships that offer larger expected improvements in their decisions.

A higher-order relationship may therefore emerge before a lower-order alternative is fully learned.

Increasing the value of a relationship while holding structural complexity fixed should affect its acquisition.

## H3 — Optimization-signal explanation

The observed learning order is more directly determined by gradient strength, representation geometry, or optimization dynamics than by either structural complexity or Bayes-level decision improvement alone.

Under this explanation, mathematically equal improvements need not produce similar learning trajectories.

## H4 — Interaction-dependent learning

Complexity, usefulness, and optimization jointly affect learning.

There may be no universal ordering rule, but restricted predictive principles may exist for identifiable classes of tasks and model architectures.

## H5 — Task-specific learning transitions

The apparent GATE staircase and leap may be specific to its generator, supervision, or architecture.

The same pattern may not occur in changed GATE variants or in T4.

These hypotheses must produce different observable predictions.

A generic claim that "all three factors matter" is not a sufficiently discriminating scientific conclusion.

# 5. WORKSTREAM A — CONSTRUCT A CONTROLLED MATHEMATICAL TASK FAMILY

**Highest priority. Mandatory.**

Construct a small, carefully designed family of GATE variants.

The purpose is to change structural complexity and decision usefulness in controlled ways.

## A1. Preserve comparability

Where possible, keep constant:

- Number of input positions.
- Total number of presented sources.
- Candidate answer vocabulary.
- Model architecture.
- Hidden dimensions.
- Training objective.
- Number of training questions.
- Data-generation budget.
- Batch size.
- Learning-rate schedule.
- Evaluation procedure.

Where a variant uses fewer gate-defining sources, use appropriately controlled irrelevant source positions rather than automatically changing the model's input size.

Ensure that these added sources do not leak trust information.

Keep the original GATE environment as a documented reference.

Do not assume a modified gate automatically has the mathematical interaction order suggested by its construction.

Verify the actual order.

## A2. Primary experimental contrasts

Build at least three mathematically validated task conditions:

| Condition | Relational complexity | Decision usefulness |
|---|---|---|
| A | Lower | Lower |
| B | Lower | Higher |
| C | Higher | Approximately matched to B |

These conditions provide two primary comparisons.

**Comparison A versus B:** Change decision usefulness while keeping structural complexity fixed.

**Comparison B versus C:** Change structural complexity while keeping decision usefulness approximately constant.

A fourth condition, higher complexity with lower usefulness, is strongly preferred if the measured training budget permits.

With four conditions, the study can examine interaction effects more directly.

With only three, report that the design consists of matched contrasts rather than a complete factorial experiment.

## A3. Candidate gate constructions

The original GATE uses a relation between two non-answer sources.

Construct a higher-complexity variant where the gate requires a genuine relation among three non-answer sources.

For example, investigate modular relationships of the form:

\[
g = f(x_0,x_1,x_2)
\]

But do not assume such a relation has the intended mathematical properties.

Verify:

- The gate's minimum required source count.
- Whether individual sources reveal trust.
- Whether pairs reveal trust.
- Its actual decision interaction order.
- The exact posterior.
- The lower-order optimal rules.
- The availability of an intermediate strategy.

If the original task requires order three and the new task requires order four, verify this formally.

A higher-order task must not accidentally become solvable through a low-order shortcut.

## A4. Change usefulness without changing interaction order

Investigate controlled changes in evidence reliability, gate relevance, or other explicitly modeled parameters.

The objective is to alter the Bayes-level value of the relation while preserving its structural interaction order.

For every candidate modification:

- Recompute the exact Bayes rule.
- Recompute the lower-order optimum.
- Recompute the full relational gain.
- Check whether source-wise trust invisibility survives.
- Check whether the decision task remains nontrivial.
- Verify that the gate is still required for the claimed optimal computation.

Avoid variants where a supposedly high-order relationship becomes unnecessary.

When matching usefulness across different interaction orders, use a preregistered numerical tolerance.

If matching exact NLL gain and accuracy gain simultaneously is impossible, prioritize exact expected NLL gain, report accuracy as a secondary difference, and acknowledge the remaining mismatch.

Do not silently label materially different tasks as usefulness-matched.

## A5. Ensure the intermediate strategy is actually available

For a meaningful test of staged learning, the intermediate computation must offer a genuine improvement.

Calculate the exact gain available to each intermediate function class.

If the intermediate gain is zero, there is no reason to expect a distinct intermediate learning stage.

If the gain is extremely small, the experiment may be unable to distinguish absence of learning from weak partial learning.

Use the mathematical calculations to assess whether the comparison is diagnostically useful.

Reject or redesign non-diagnostic variants during development.

## A6. Required mathematical output

For every retained condition, produce:

- Exact generator definition.
- Trust relation.
- Proof or verified argument for minimal relational arity.
- Proof or verified argument for minimal decision interaction order.
- Exact Bayes rule.
- Order-restricted optimal rules.
- Expected NLL for each relevant order.
- Expected accuracy for each relevant order.
- Incremental decision benefit.
- Valid counterfactual relational edits.
- Expected outputs of the proposed learning-stage diagnostics.

Provide executable verification.

A variant is not eligible for confirmation training until its mathematical references have passed independent checks.

# 6. WORKSTREAM B — MAKE LEARNING-ORDER PREDICTIONS BEFORE TRAINING

**Mandatory.**

This is the scientific center of XV.

The mathematical task family must lead to quantitative predictions about learning trajectories.

## B1. Define the observable stages

Use exact decision references rather than broad descriptions such as "the model starts learning relations."

Define measurable states corresponding to:

**Source-based behavior**

The model is primarily using individual-source evidence and has not acquired the diagnostic relational response.

**Intermediate relational behavior**

The model uses a relevant lower-order relationship but has not acquired the complete higher-order trust rule.

**Full relational behavior**

The model responds to the full trust relation and approaches the appropriate decision rule.

A model may move between these states continuously.

Do not force every trajectory into a categorical staircase.

Preserve continuous measurements.

## B2. Construct calibrated diagnostics

For the original GATE, XIV used:

- Gate-flip response.
- Pairwise-agreement response.
- Exact reference NLL values.
- Accuracy.
- Dense training checkpoints.

Reuse these diagnostics where mathematically valid.

For new variants, derive the appropriate analogues.

In particular, a higher-order gate may require new counterfactual edits to distinguish partial relational learning from full rule acquisition.

Each diagnostic must have reference responses for:

- Order-1 behavior.
- Relevant intermediate optimal behavior.
- Bayes-optimal behavior.
- Non-relational or shortcut alternatives.

Avoid interpreting an accuracy plateau alone as evidence of a specific internal computation.

Different algorithms may achieve the same accuracy.

## B3. Define a learning transition

Predefine:

- The relevant response threshold.
- The minimum persistence across checkpoints.
- How borderline scores are handled.
- How temporary improvement is distinguished from stable acquisition.
- How abruptness is measured.
- What counts as skipping an intermediate stage.
- What counts as failing to learn the task.

Use sufficient checkpoint density to resolve transitions.

A jump must be distinguished from a gradual change missed because checkpoints were too far apart.

Where feasible, store snapshots approximately every 25 steps during the early learning period, matching XIV's successful approach.

Adapt snapshot spacing only on the basis of independent development measurements.

## B4. Freeze competing predictions

At minimum, construct predictions addressing:

**P1 — Complexity contrast**

With usefulness approximately matched, does the higher-order relation emerge later, less reliably, or through a different sequence?

**P2 — Usefulness contrast**

With structural complexity fixed, does increasing decision benefit change the timing or ordering of relational acquisition?

**P3 — Intermediate-stage prediction**

Under which exact payoff conditions should the model learn a lower-order relationship before acquiring the full relation?

**P4 — Leap prediction**

Under which conditions should the model acquire the full relational response before substantially mastering the intermediate strategy?

**P5 — Learning-signal alternative**

Do mathematical benefit and structural complexity retain predictive value after accounting for measured optimization signals, or do learning-signal properties better explain the trajectories?

**P6 — Cross-environment prediction**

If T4 trainability is established, does an appropriately defined learning-stage pattern occur there under its own mathematical references?

Do not automatically treat these candidate predictions as six valid frozen tests.

Develop the quantitative thresholds and confirm each prediction has the ability to distinguish competing hypotheses.

Prefer fewer meaningful frozen tests over many redundant checks.
