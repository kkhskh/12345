# 2. THE SIX MAIN OPEN SCIENTIFIC PROBLEMS

The previous submission left six connected problems.

## Problem A — The transferred computation is not causally identified

P-count pretraining recovers approximately 88% of the observed T1→T4 curriculum advantage.

But:

- P-count explicitly supervises leave-question agreement counts.
- The original frozen feature probe examined source-token representations.
- The relevant counts were later decoded perfectly at readout tokens.
- Feature location differs between P-count and T1.
- Probe accuracy does not establish causal necessity.
- Only one seed per pretraining arm was evaluated.
- The early-learning timeline is confounded by the 1,000-step fine-tuning protocol.

We know that relational pretraining is useful in the tested configuration.

We do not yet understand exactly what transferable computation it creates.

## Problem B — GATE's actual causal pathway is incompletely reconstructed

GATE is a second learned relation-sensitive decision mechanism outside Bayesian latent reliability.

The relation is a modular compatibility constraint between non-answer sources.

The learned model reaches approximately 0.900 accuracy against Bayes 0.899.

But the visually salient last-layer relation-dependent attention is causally dispensable.

Removing every last-layer attention-head output leaves performance essentially unchanged.

The actual pathway appears to involve:

- Earlier relation-dependent evidence-token computation.
- Integration into the readout residual.
- Subsequent residual and MLP computation.
- Candidate-specific output.

This must be reconstructed more precisely.

We must not mistake a correlated attention pattern for the mechanism.

## Problem C — The prospective internal predictor of extrapolation failed

The frozen P-A predictor had held-out error 0.126 against an architecture-only baseline of 0.091.

It also ranked the held-out Transformers incorrectly.

This is a real failed prediction.

It is not evidence that internal structure can never predict extrapolation.

But we must stop treating attractive internal signatures as explanatory unless they predict something beyond architectural and behavioral baselines.

## Problem D — TRANS composition was not adequately investigated

The TRANS setting involves episode-shared latent transformations σ and τ.

The model showed partial one-hop learning:

- AB: 0.933 versus Bayes 0.952.
- BC: 0.629 versus Bayes 0.653.
- C-only: 0.336 versus Bayes 0.487.

It was stopped after 4,000 training steps while learning was still developing.

The model did not meaningfully use C reports in the recorded checks.

It remains unknown whether:

- σ was fully learned.
- τ was encoded.
- The transformations were composed.
- The network could learn composition with more training.
- The observed failure is optimization, representation, or task-structure dependent.

This entire mathematical and computational direction remains open.

## Problem E — We do not have a nontrivial common causal class

T4 and GATE both use source relationships to affect candidate decisions.

But they use different computational pathways.

T4 contains reliability-sensitive attention routing.

GATE does not require its final-layer routing at all.

TRANS may eventually provide a third mechanism involving composition rather than source weighting.

We need to determine whether these mechanisms share an identifiable mathematical or causal structure, or whether they are genuinely distinct classes.

Do not assume they unify.

Do not reject unification merely because their internal components differ.

## Problem F — Important generality investigations were never executed

The latest report explicitly left unfinished:

- Additional independent seeds for the pretraining experiments.
- Longer fine-tuning checks needed to remove the learning-dynamics confound.
- A test of whether TRANS represents τ.
- Adaptive sources.
- Learned models under changing or unknown source groups.
- Partial copying and collusion regimes.
- Broader architecture and candidate settings.

These omissions must be incorporated into the research.

They are not optional footnotes.

# 5. WORKSTREAM B — COMPLETE THE GATE MECHANISM

The current GATE result is not yet a fully reconstructed circuit.

The task defines a non-answer relation:

x₀ + x₁ ≡ 0 (mod K).

That relationship determines which answer-bearing evidence pair is informative.

The model reaches Bayes-level accuracy.

The original causal observations include:

- Removing layer-0 attention outputs reduces accuracy to approximately 0.691.
- Removing layer-1 attention outputs reduces it to approximately 0.881.
- Removing all layer-2 attention outputs leaves accuracy approximately 0.900.
- Patching evidence-token states at layer-1 input transfers a substantial gate-dependent effect.
- Patching the full readout residual at layer-2 input transfers almost the entire effect.
- Patching the layer-2 query alone does not.

These observations locate a causal dependency but do not fully explain its computation.

## B1. Reconstruct the forward mechanism

Trace the actual computational pathway from:

- Gate-source embeddings.
- Gate-source compatibility.
- Evidence-token transformations.
- Layer-0 attention and output projections.
- Layer-1 attention.
- Intermediate MLP computation.
- Readout residual.
- Layer-2 MLP and skip connection.
- Final candidate logits.

Include the full Q/K/V/O, normalization, and residual computation.

Do not focus on attention patterns alone.

Identify what operation actually makes the candidate decision conditional on the gate relation.

## B2. Separate information transmission from decision computation

There may be distinct stages:

1. Representing the modular relation.
2. Transmitting relation information to evidence tokens.
3. Combining that information with candidate-bearing evidence.
4. Producing candidate margins.

Determine whether the model stores a gating variable or computes a more direct candidate-specific representation.

A feature that decodes the gate but never affects the decision is not a mediator.

A readout residual encoding the final answer is not automatically a separate compatibility representation.

## B3. Identify necessary and sufficient pathways

Use:

- Path patching.
- Targeted attention-head interventions.
- MLP interventions.
- Readout-residual interventions.
- Matched-state controls.
- Negative-control sources.
- Selective gate changes while answer evidence remains fixed.

Compare interventions at the gate-source, evidence-token, and readout sites.

Test whether a small computational subgraph is sufficient for the gate-dependent candidate response.

Also investigate redundant or compensating pathways.

Ablating a full layer may change output for unrelated reasons, so use finer interventions and matched controls.

## B4. Generalize GATE

Train additional seeds.

Investigate:

- Larger K.
- Different modular constraints.
- Nonlinear compatibility rules.
- Different numbers of candidate-evidence pairs.
- Multiple simultaneous gates.
- Noisy gate observations.
- Contradictory or partially informative gating sources.
- Gate relations that change across questions.
- Compositions of several compatibility conditions.

For each variant, derive the correct reference decision first.

Determine whether the learned computation remains similar or changes.

Do not interpret another successful gate classifier as proof of a shared mechanism.

## B5. Compare with reliability routing

Determine whether GATE and T4 share:

- An abstract intermediate variable.
- A conditional evidence-selection operation.
- A causally identified factorization.
- A candidate-specific response rule.
- Or only broad input/output dependence on source relationships.

Construct counterexamples to overly broad definitions.

**Required deliverable:** a reproducible, causally verified computational reconstruction of GATE, with multiple learned implementations and a precise comparison against T4.

# 8. WORKSTREAM E — FORMALIZE THE POSSIBLE COMMON MATHEMATICAL STRUCTURE

This is the central synthesis problem.

We now have three different experimental families:

1. T4: latent reliability and agreement-sensitive evidence weighting.
2. GATE: compatibility-conditioned relevance of candidate evidence.
3. TRANS: composition of latent transformations, with the learned mechanism still unresolved.

We should not force them into a single theory.

But we must seriously investigate whether a useful shared structure exists.

## E1. Define the candidate class carefully

A tentative abstract pattern is:

Independent source variables
→ a relation among those sources
→ a change in how candidate-bearing evidence is interpreted or transformed
→ a candidate-specific response.

This is only a hypothesis.

It may be too broad.

Define meaningful restrictions.

For example:

- The relation must be specified independently of the final candidate logits.
- The relation's information must be independently identifiable from the source semantics.
- A computational operation must actually depend on the relation.
- A valid causal intervention must establish its use.
- A candidate-margin consequence must be specified.
- There must be at least one falsifiable distinction from an ordinary aggregate-only computation.

If these restrictions are insufficient, strengthen or replace them based on explicit counterexamples.

## E2. Compare computational factorizations

For T4, determine whether the computation factors through reliability information.

For GATE, determine whether it factors through compatibility-conditioned evidence selection.

For TRANS, investigate whether it factors through identified transformations and their composition.

The mathematical object may differ between tasks.

Determine whether an abstraction can represent those differences without becoming trivial.

Consider possible connections to:

- Conditional evidence transformation.
- Causal abstraction.
- Sufficient-statistic computation.
- Information routing.
- Constraint-based inference.
- Compositional operators.

Do not invent terminology as a substitute for proving structure.

## E3. Find implementation-independent properties

Investigate whether different implementations preserve:

- Input-level relational dependence.
- Specific intervention-response relationships.
- Candidate-margin constraints.
- Information sufficiency.
- Causal factorization.
- Abstract mediator behavior.
- Transformation composition rules.
- Certain invariants under function-preserving reparameterization.

A universal property must be tested across genuinely different implementation families.

A property that follows only from identical Bayes outputs should not be presented as new mechanistic structure.

## E4. Prove restricted positive theorems

Attempt the strongest defensible theorem family.

Each theorem must specify:

- Model class.
- Source semantics.
- Relation definition.
- Computational operation.
- Intervention family.
- Candidate-specific readout.
- Competing causal paths.
- Signed assumptions.
- Exact conclusion.

Do not prove merely that a nonlinear network can compute an arbitrary function.

Do not embed the desired candidate-margin sign inside the assumptions.

## E5. Construct adversarial counterexamples

Attack the candidate abstraction using:

- Direct candidate classification without an explicit mediator.
- Distributed computation.
- Redundant evidence pathways.
- Alternative representations.
- Same relation with opposite candidate decisions.
- Same task behavior with different internal circuits.

## E6. Do not force TRANS into a class before it has been learned
