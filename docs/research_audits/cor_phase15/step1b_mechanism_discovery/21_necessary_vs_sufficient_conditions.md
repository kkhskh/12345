# 21 — Necessary and sufficient conditions

## Necessary, in the unrestricted linear class

N1: no function of a linear `Q` recovers `sign(L(Δ))` for every linear `L` with `L(Δ)≠0` when `Δ ∈ ker Q`. Instances of that kernel pattern: CE-08, probe-preserving kernel edits. CE-01 is the same idea with the readout flipped at a fixed cochain, not a displacement in `H`; it is a sibling, not a substitution instance.

N1 is not a necessity for matching a sign on a domain that contains only one sign. Sufficient conditions below are for other predicates (`ΔL=w·δ`, vanishing `I`) and are not the converse of N1.

Further necessities, as candidate claims with proofs in restricted classes:

- The intervention family must be specified. Sufficiency is family-relative.
- Maps used to form `Q` must be frozen without evaluation `R` if the claim is a forecast.
- Pairwise parent equalities must not replace the true aggregator.
- Euclidean interpretation requires a declared metric, which LN already changes.

These are necessities for a relational predictor to even be in the running. They are not a mechanism.

## Sufficient, and much stronger

`L = q(Q)` on the domain, plus a specified monotone relation, would make `Q` determine `L`. That sufficient condition is nearly the conclusion. The interesting sufficient conditions that are not tautological are:

- Linear residual readout: `ΔL = w·δ` (sufficient to sign a known `δ`; uses `w`).
- Known linear next map of a sum: `I=0` (sufficient to predict vanishing interaction; uses architecture).
- Known square bottleneck: `I=2` at the unit parallel clamps in the test (uses the functional form of `L`).

The gap between necessary (N1) and these sufficient conditions is the whole identification problem: one must independently get `w`, or the next map, or `q`. Getting them from evaluation responses is not a bridge. Getting `w` from the architecture is attribution.

A theory that applies only when `L` already minimizes obstruction is too narrow to explain Transformer decisions, and is not claimed.
