# 23 — Step 1C verdict

**Verdict:** `STEP1C_NECESSARY_CONDITIONS_ADVANCED`

The main result is a sharper account of what a relational statistic must satisfy before it can constrain a response, together with the removal of four false sentences from Step 1B. No computation-grounded COR candidate was identified. Every positive identity that remains is ordinary calculus, the definition of sufficiency, or the attention formula. That fact is why the verdict is not `STEP1C_COMPUTATION_GROUNDED_CANDIDATE_IDENTIFIED` and not `STEP1C_CONDITIONAL_MECHANISM_SURVIVES`.

It is also why the verdict is not `STEP1C_ONLY_GENERIC_EXISTING_RESULTS_SURVIVE` as the label for the whole step. The generic identities are real, and `22` classifies them as generic. The step's own addition is the boundary: which quantifiers were wrong, which operator has to be the kernel, which intervention the interaction statement is about, and which information a residual does not contain. Using the generic-only label would hide those boundaries. Using a candidate label would hide that the boundaries do not yield a new mechanism.

`STEP1C_NO_SURVIVING_CANDIDATE_IN_EXAMINED_CLASSES` would fit the relational scores and would not fit the necessary conditions, which do survive. `STEP1C_INCOMPLETE_MATHEMATICAL_INVESTIGATION` would fit a record that still attributed the interaction theorems to the pre-norm block or still reversed the fiber quantifiers. Those sentences were repaired before this verdict. The unwritten multi-layer, post-norm, rotary, and full RMSNorm-block cases stay unresolved in `19`. They are not missing premises of this label.

`PHASE_2 = NO GO`. `OBSTRUCTION_READY = NO`. `COR_PHASE1_CONDITIONAL_THEORY_REPAIR_REQUIRED` is unchanged. Direction 1 is unchanged. No Transformer was run.

## Questions

**Q1.** The universal claims `D\downarrow\Rightarrow L\uparrow` and `D_c<D_{c'}\Rightarrow L_c>L_{c'}` are false. So is the claim that a quadratic obstruction is a causal mechanism, that an arbitrary `\rho` is a transport of the computation, that a projected representation is automatically sufficient, and that the historical Othello obstruction evaluations established the mechanism.

**Q2.** Unproved, not disproved: whether some restriction outside the examined classes, checkable without `sign(R)`, implies fiber constancy for a statistic that is not already the Jacobian or the full state. Multi-layer re-entry is in that class. The nonlinear extension of N1 remains a conjecture.

**Q3.** N1: if the domain contains both orientations of a displacement in `\ker Q` and `L` is linear and nonzero on it, no function of `(Q(h),Q(h+\Delta))` recovers both signs. The kernel has to be the kernel of the statistic that is actually used.

**Q4.** Step 1B did not derive the claims from `Q`, `K`, `V`, the residual equation, or LayerNorm as a computation. It also stated four false generalities, corrected in `01`.

**Q5.** The skip adds the residual to a branch that reads `LN` of that residual. An edit of the residual is not an edit of an independent addend. Attention is linear in `V` only while `A` is held fixed. A residual edit moves `Q`, `K`, and `V` together, and in a pre-norm block it moves them through `LN`. An MLP is affine on a fixed activation piece and can be nonlinear and separable across channels. LayerNorm changes the vector a fixed readout sees and need not preserve its sign. The logit is a function of the final residual through that normalization, not of an arbitrary projection.

**Q6.** A projection determines a response on a named family exactly when the response is constant on its fibers in that family.

**Q7.** In the two-token example, the discarded coordinate is a query and a key feature. The values match. The outputs are `1/2` and `1/(1+e^{16})`.

**Q8.** Yes, as a joint measurement. The sufficient object is the tuple, or the concatenation. That is N2, kept.

**Q9.** No. A relation is a function of the combined values. It can destroy information. It cannot supply information the tuple does not have.

**Q10.** Not from the residual alone. Surrogate error, a dropped coordinate, a missing parent, a linear `\rho` on a nonlinear map, a clamp, and a nonzero interaction of the downstream map can produce the same symptom. The corrected kernel statement says which operator has to be held fixed before a direction is called invisible. It does not name the source.

**Q11.** When the child is assigned by the structural equation. The residual is then zero for every such intervention. A clamp that copies the structural value also gives zero, so zero does not identify the intervention.

**Q12.** A projected equation can be inexact and still describe a derivative, if the offsets match and the remainder is controlled. Inexactness alone is not information about `R`. The fiber theorem still applies to whatever number is computed.

**Q13.** A `\rho` that is not allowed to depend on the target of the pair. The rank-one formula shows what happens when it is allowed to. Fixing `\rho` without the target makes the residual a definite number. It does not make the number a cause.

**Q14.** A proof that, under hypotheses that do not mention `R`, the residual's level sets are the level sets of the claimed property of `R`. No examined residual has that proof.

**Q15.** Through the downstream equation: a linear form on a sum, a pattern times values, or an MLP of the sum. The total derivative sums the paths the intervention does not clamp.

**Q16.** In the linear sum, when each remaining inner product after a single deletion has the margin's sign. Vector agreement is not that condition.

**Q17.** When a structural equation recomputes one contribution after another is removed, and the recomputed value restores the margin. A linear sum does not do this. Softmax moves weight because the row sums to one, in the two-point case, or by the softmax Jacobian. That is not a repair circuit.

**Q18.** Compensation is a change in another equation after the intervention. Ordinary nonlinearity is the shape of the equation that was already there. A nonzero interaction shows the equation is nonseparable on that pair. It does not show a second mechanism.

**Q19.** When the four-term difference of the named pair, at the named baseline, is nonzero. For `C^2` maps, the interaction function is not identically zero exactly when the map is not separable on the rectangle.

**Q20.** Because a nonlinear map can be a sum of functions of each argument. `u^2+v^2` has interaction zero everywhere. Because a nonseparable map can still be zero at the pair that was evaluated. `(u+v)^3` at `(1,-1)`.

**Q21.** Only if the relation is a sufficient statistic for the interaction and was fixed without evaluating it. The clamped-pattern law predicts interaction zero without a second evaluation, and the information it uses is the attention equation, not a residual. No residual examined here determines the interaction of a residual edit.

**Q22.** The examined relations do not determine sign once both orientations sit in a fiber. They also do not determine magnitude beyond a bound that actually comes from a Lipschitz constant or a Jacobian.

**Q23.** Fiber constancy of the sign on the domain where the prediction is claimed; the intervention named in the theorem; and the kernel of the statistic that is actually computed. Assuming the sign agrees with the score is not among them.

**Q24.** The value-only response equals the Jacobian-vector product of that clamped family, because the map is linear. A relational residual does not appear in the identity.

**Q25.** Not in the examined families, beyond writing down that Jacobian. Knowing only that the edit is value-only yields remainder zero and not the numerical response. That is the attention equation again.

**Q26.** The clamp, or the weights that make the pattern independent of the edit, are checkable without `R`. They were checked as formulas. They do not identify a relational score.

**Q27.** Nothing, in the examined families. A path effect is the intervention with the complementary edges clamped. The closed form for values is linearity.

**Q28.** Nothing beyond the definition of exact sufficiency for a function `P`. An arbitrary `\rho` is an alignment that has not been shown to preserve `R`. Unrestricted alignments can manufacture the preservation.

**Q29.** The implemented nodes and the responses under a stated `do` are defined by the block. `P`, `\rho`, `W`, and `M` are defined by the analyst. `O` and `D` depend on that choice. `O` does not depend on `M`.

**Q30.** A dual reparameterization preserves the scalars it transforms. LayerNorm as computation does not. A positive ReLU rescaling preserves the bias-free function and can change a hidden score on the active set.

**Q31.** Yes. The bias-free ReLU pair implements one function and two hidden vectors at the tested input.
